Gambling Strategies & Fairness

My understanding, built from scratch.

1. The Game Process ($\Xi_n$)

Let $\eta_n$ be the winnings/losses per unit stake in game $n$.
Total winnings after $n$ games with a unit stake: $\Xi_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \eta_k$.
Information available after $n$ games: $\mathcal{F}_n = \sigma(\eta_1, \ldots, \eta_n)$.
ConditionTypeMeaning
$E(\Xi_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) = \Xi_{n-1}$MartingaleFair game (expected change = 0)
$E(\Xi_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \geq \Xi_{n-1}$Sub-MartingaleFavorable to you (expected change $\geq 0$)
$E(\Xi_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \leq \Xi_{n-1}$Super-MartingaleUnfavorable to you (expected change $\leq 0$)

2. The Gambling Strategy ($\alpha_n$)

In game $n$, your stake is $\alpha_n$.
Crucial Condition: $\alpha_n$ must be $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$-measurable (predictable).
⚠️ If $\alpha_n$ were $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable, you would be placing bets after knowing the outcome (crystal ball / insider trading). The math requires predictable strategies to model reality correctly.

3. Total Wealth Process ($Z_n$)

Your total wealth after $n$ games with strategy $\alpha$: $$Z_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k \eta_k = \sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k (\Xi_k - \Xi_{k-1})$$ Recursive form: $Z_n = Z_{n-1} + \alpha_n(\Xi_n - \Xi_{n-1})$.

4. Preservation Theorem

Part 1 (Martingale): If the game is fair ($\Xi_n$ is MG) and your strategy $\alpha$ is bounded, then your total wealth $Z_n$ is also a MG.
(You cannot invent a betting system to beat a fair casino).

Proof: $$E(Z_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) = E\big( Z_{n-1} + \alpha_n(\Xi_n - \Xi_{n-1}) \;\big|\; \mathcal{F}_{n-1} \big)$$ Since $Z_{n-1}$ and $\alpha_n$ are $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$-measurable, they pull out: $$= Z_{n-1} + \alpha_n \cdot \underbrace{E( \Xi_n - \Xi_{n-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1})}_{=0 \text{ (since } \Xi \text{ is MG)}}$$ $$= Z_{n-1} \quad \checkmark$$
Part 2 (Sub-Martingale): If the game is favorable ($\Xi_n$ is Sub-MG) and your strategy is bounded and non-negative ($\alpha_n \geq 0$), then $Z_n$ is also a Sub-MG.

Why $\alpha_n \geq 0$?
If $\Xi_n$ is a Sub-MG, $E(\Xi_n - \Xi_{n-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \geq 0$.
If you were allowed to take a negative position (short selling, $\alpha_n < 0$), you would multiply a negative stake by a positive expected gain, resulting in an expected loss! To maintain the favorable property, you must stick to long positions.
