28 — Hitting Times, Reflection Principle & Running Maximum

Wiener Process Stopping Times, Strong Markov Symmetry, and Running Max Distributions.

1. Hitting Time Definition

For standard Brownian motion $W(t)$, the first passage time (hitting time) to level $a$ is: $$T_a = \inf\{t \ge 0 : W(t) = a\}$$ $T_a$ is a stopping time because its occurrence depends only on path history up to time $t$.

2. The Reflection Principle & Strong Markov Property

For $a > 0$, partition the probability of being above $a$ at time $t$: $$\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) = \mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a \mid T_a \le t)\mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) + \mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a \mid T_a > t)\mathbb{P}(T_a > t)$$
  • $\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a \mid T_a > t) = 0$ (must be below $a$ if it never hit $a$).
  • By the **Strong Markov Property**, the post-$T_a$ process $B(u) = W(T_a + u) - W(T_a)$ is a standard Brownian motion independent of history. Because standard Brownian motion is symmetric: $$\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a \mid T_a \le t) = \mathbb{P}(B(t-T_a) \ge 0) = \frac{1}{2}$$
$$\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) \implies \mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) = 2\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a)$$

3. Probability Distribution of $T_a$

Using standard Gaussian integration with substitution $y = x / \sqrt{t}$: $$\mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{|a|/\sqrt{t}}^{\infty} e^{-\frac{y^2}{2}} \, dy = 2\left[ 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{|a|}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \right]$$ Differentiating the CDF with respect to $t$ gives the PDF of $T_a$ (Lévy distribution): $$f(t) = \frac{|a|}{\sqrt{2\pi t^3}} e^{-\frac{a^2}{2t}} \quad (t > 0)$$

4. The Running Maximum $M(t)$

Define the running maximum up to time $t$ as: $$M(t) = \max_{0 \le s \le t} W(s)$$ The event that the maximum is at least $a$ is equivalent to the hitting time being at or before $t$: $$\{M(t) \ge a\} \iff \{T_a \le t\}$$ $$\mathbb{P}(M(t) \ge a) = 2\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) = 2\left[ 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \right] \quad (a > 0)$$

5. Certain Hitting Time ($\mathbb{P}(T_a < \infty) = 1$)

$$\mathbb{P}(T_a < \infty) = \lim_{t \to \infty} \mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) = \lim_{t \to \infty} 2\left[ 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{|a|}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \right] = 2\left[ 1 - \Phi(0) \right] = 2\left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = 1$$ Standard Brownian motion will hit any level $a$ almost surely, though the expected time $\mathbb{E}[T_a] = \infty$.
