Starting Pieces — Probability Theory

My understanding, built from scratch.

1. The Outcome

The experiment runs. One outcome is produced. Call it $\omega$.
$\Omega$ = the set of all possible outcomes (sample space).
$\omega \in \Omega$ always.

2. Events

An event is just a subset $A \subseteq \Omega$.

$A$ happened iff $\omega \in A$. (Did the actual outcome land inside the set?)
$A$ did not happen iff $\omega \notin A$.
Examples (coin toss forever):
  • $A_H = \{\omega : \text{first toss is H}\}$
  • $A_{HH} = \{\omega : \text{first two tosses are HH}\}$
  • $\Omega$ = always happens. $\varnothing$ = never happens.

3. σ-field (Sigma-Algebra)

A σ-field $\mathcal{F}$ is a collection of subsets of $\Omega$ that we are allowed to assign probabilities to.

Must satisfy:
  1. $\varnothing \in \mathcal{F}$
  2. $A \in \mathcal{F} \implies A^c \in \mathcal{F}$ (if you can ask "did $A$?", you can ask "did NOT $A$?")
  3. $A_1, A_2, \ldots \in \mathcal{F} \implies \bigcup_i A_i \in \mathcal{F}$
Just accept these axioms. They exist so probability arithmetic never breaks.

4. Special Events & Filtration

At time $k$, you have seen $\omega_1, \omega_2, \ldots, \omega_k$.

$A$ is a special event at time $k$ if: knowing $\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_k$ is enough to say definitively — $\omega \in A$ or $\omega \notin A$.

$A$ is NOT special at time $k$ if: you need more tosses. You cannot decide yet.
EventSpecial at $k=1$?Reason
$A_H$✅ YesSaw $\omega_1$, enough to decide
$A_{HH}$❌ NoNeed $\omega_2$
$\Omega$✅ YesAlways yes, needs 0 tosses
$\mathcal{F}_k$ = the set of ALL special events at time $k$.

As $k$ grows, you see more → more events become special: $$\mathcal{F}_0 \subset \mathcal{F}_1 \subset \mathcal{F}_2 \subset \cdots$$ This chain is the filtration. It tracks your growing information over time. $\mathcal{F}_k$ represents all information available by time $k$.

5. Probability Measure

$P$ is a function: $P : \mathcal{F} \to [0,1]$.
Input: an event. Output: a number.

Two rules:
  1. $P(\Omega) = 1$
  2. If $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ are pairwise disjoint: $P\left(\bigcup_i A_i\right) = \sum_i P(A_i)$

6. Probability Space

$(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ — the three pieces:
  • $\Omega$ — all possible outcomes
  • $\mathcal{F}$ — the events we are allowed to assign probabilities to
  • $P$ — the function that assigns those numbers

7. Almost Surely (a.s.)

$P(A) = 1$ → $A$ occurs almost surely. NOT "surely."

Surely: $A = \Omega$, impossible to be outside $A$.
Almost surely: $P(A) = 1$, but exceptions of probability 0 may exist.
