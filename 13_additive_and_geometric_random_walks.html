13 — Additive & Geometric Random Walks

Rigorously deriving the martingale conditions for discrete additive random walks and exponential (geometric) random walks.

Step 1: Additive Random Walk

Definition: Let (Yⱼ)j ≥ 1 be i.i.d. random variables with E[|Y₁|] < ∞.
Define X₀ = 0 and Xₙ = ∑j=1ⁿ Yⱼ for n ≥ 1, with natural filtration ℱₙ = σ(Y₁, …, Yₙ).

Derivation of E[ Xₙ₊₁ | ℱₙ ]:
1. Split: Xₙ₊₁ = Xₙ + Yₙ₊₁.
2. Linearity: E[ Xₙ₊₁ | ℱₙ ] = E[ Xₙ | ℱₙ ] + E[ Yₙ₊₁ | ℱₙ ].
3. Measurability & Independence: Since Xₙ is ℱₙ-measurable and Yₙ₊₁ is independent of ℱₙ:

  E[ Xₙ₊₁ | ℱₙ ] = Xₙ + E[ Y₁ ]

Conclusion:
• (Xₙ) is a martingale ⟺ E[ Y₁ ] = 0.
• (Xₙ) is a submartingale ⟺ E[ Y₁ ] ≥ 0.
• (Xₙ) is a supermartingale ⟺ E[ Y₁ ] ≤ 0.

Step 2: Geometric (Exponential) Random Walk

Definition: Let (Yⱼ)j ≥ 1 be i.i.d. random variables and X₀ > 0 constant.
Define the geometric random walk:

  Xₙ = X₀ eY₁ + … + Yₙ = Xₙ₋₁ eYₙ    (n ≥ 1)

with natural filtration ℱₙ = σ(Y₁, …, Yₙ).

Derivation of E[ Xₙ₊₁ | ℱₙ ]:
1. Product Form: Xₙ₊₁ = Xₙ · eYₙ₊₁.
2. Taking Out What Is Known: Since Xₙ is ℱₙ-measurable:
  E[ Xₙ₊₁ | ℱₙ ] = Xₙ · E[ eYₙ₊₁ | ℱₙ ].
3. Independence Rule: Since Yₙ₊₁ is independent of ℱₙ:

  E[ Xₙ₊₁ | ℱₙ ] = Xₙ · E[ eY₁ ] = Xₙ · ϕY(1)

Conclusion: (Xₙ) is a martingale ⟺ E[ eY₁ ] = 1 (i.e. ϕY(1) = 1).

Step 3: Normal Log-Increments Case

Special Case (Log-Normal): If Yⱼ ~ N(μ, σ²) i.i.d., then its Moment Generating Function is:

  E[ eY₁ ] = eμ + ½ σ²

For Xₙ = X₀ e∑ Yⱼ to be a martingale:

  eμ + ½ σ² = 1 ⟹ μ = - ½ σ²

This exact drift correction (μ = -½σ²) appears fundamentally in continuous-time geometric Brownian motion and Black-Scholes asset modeling.
