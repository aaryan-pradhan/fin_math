23 — Wiener Process Axioms, Lévy Characterization & Random Walk Limit

Constructing Brownian Motion from Axioms, Martingales, and Discrete Limits.

1. Classical Definition of Wiener Process

A stochastic process $\{W(t), t \ge 0\}$ is a Standard Wiener Process (Brownian Motion) iff:
  1. $W(0) = 0$ almost surely (a.s.).
  2. Sample paths $t \mapsto W(t, \omega)$ are continuous a.s.
  3. Increments are independent and stationary.
  4. For $0 \le s < t$, increments are Gaussian: $W(t) - W(s) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$.

2. Lévy's Martingale Characterization

Without assuming independent or Gaussian increments directly, a continuous process $W(t)$ with $W(0) = 0$ is a Wiener process iff:
  • $W(t)$ is a martingale wrt natural filtration $\mathcal{F}_t$: $\mathbb{E}[W(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = W(s)$.
  • $W(t)^2 - t$ is a martingale wrt $\mathcal{F}_t$: $\mathbb{E}[W(t)^2 - t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = W(s)^2 - s$.
Conditional Variance Derivation: $$\mathbb{E}\left[(W(t) - W(s))^2 \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_s\right] = \mathbb{E}[W(t)^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_s] - 2W(s)\mathbb{E}[W(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s] + W(s)^2 = (W(s)^2 + t - s) - 2W(s)^2 + W(s)^2 = t - s$$

3. Microscopic Random Walk Construction

Consider a discrete symmetric random walk with step size $\Delta x$ every time step $\Delta t$: $$X(t) = \Delta x \sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor t / \Delta t \rfloor} X_i \quad \text{where } X_i = \pm 1 \text{ with equal prob } \frac{1}{2}$$ $$\mathbb{E}[X(t)] = 0, \quad \text{Var}(X(t)) = (\Delta x)^2 \cdot \frac{t}{\Delta t}$$

4. The Diffusion Scaling Limit & CLT

If $\Delta x = \Delta t \to 0$, $\text{Var}(X(t)) = t \Delta t \to 0$ (motion collapses).
To preserve non-zero variance as step size vanishes, we must scale spatial steps as: $$\Delta x = \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t} \implies \text{Var}(X(t)) \to \sigma^2 t$$
By the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), as $\Delta t \to 0$ ($N = \frac{t}{\Delta t} \to \infty$), the standardized sum $\frac{S_N}{\sqrt{N}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.
Multiplying by $c = \sigma \sqrt{t}$ scales the variance by $c^2 = \sigma^2 t$: $$X(t) = \sigma \sqrt{t} \cdot \left( \frac{S_N}{\sqrt{N}} \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 t)$$
