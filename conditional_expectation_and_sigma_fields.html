Conditional Expectation & σ-Fields

Rigorously connecting independence, sub-σ-fields, the Law of Iterated Expectations, and operational rules.

Step 1: Independence of Random Variables & σ-Fields

Independent σ-Fields: Two sub-σ-fields $\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ are independent if for all $A \in \mathcal{G}$ and all $B \in \mathcal{H}$: $$P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$$ Independent Random Variables: $\xi$ and $\eta$ are independent $\iff$ their generated σ-fields $\sigma(\xi)$ and $\sigma(\eta)$ are independent, where: $$\sigma(\xi) = \{\xi^{-1}(B) \subseteq \Omega : B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\}$$

Step 2: What is $E(X \mid \mathcal{G})$?

$E(X \mid \mathcal{G})$ is a RANDOM VARIABLE, not a single number.
  • $E(X \mid Y = y)$ is a single real number (the estimate when $Y(\omega) = y$).
  • $E(X \mid Y) = E(X \mid \sigma(Y))$ is a random variable that assigns $E(X \mid Y = y)$ whenever $Y(\omega) = y$.
  • $E(X \mid \mathcal{G})$ is the general random variable for any sub-σ-field $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$.
Formal Definition: $Y = E(X \mid \mathcal{G})$ is the unique (a.s.) random variable satisfying:
  1. $Y$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable: $\sigma(Y) \subseteq \mathcal{G}$. $Y$ cannot see finer details than $\mathcal{G}$ allows and is forced to be constant on every information block $G \in \mathcal{G}$.
  2. Integral Matching (for all $G \in \mathcal{G}$): $\int_G Y\, dP = \int_G X\, dP$. Since $Y$ is constant ($c_G$) on block $G$, this reduces to $c_G \cdot P(G) = \int_G X\, dP \implies c_G = \int_G X\, dP \,/\, P(G)$. Condition 2 forces $Y$ to equal the exact average of $X$ over each block $G$.
  3. Unique (a.s.): Any two random variables $Y_1, Y_2$ satisfying (1) & (2) must be equal everywhere except on a set of probability zero ($P(Y_1 \neq Y_2) = 0$).

Step 3: Law of Iterated Expectations (Tower Property)

Theorem: $E(E(X \mid Y)) = E(X)$ and generally $E(E(X \mid \mathcal{G})) = E(X)$.

Proof via Joint Densities: $$\begin{aligned} E(E(X \mid Y)) &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\, f_{X|Y=y}(x)\, dx\right] f_Y(y)\, dy \\ &= \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} x\, f_{X|Y=y}(x)\, f_Y(y)\, dx\, dy \\ &= \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} x\, f(x, y)\, dx\, dy \\ &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x \left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x, y)\, dy\right] dx = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\, f_X(x)\, dx = E(X) \end{aligned}$$
Tail Probability Application: Setting $X = \mathbb{1}_{\{X > t\}}$ gives $P(X > t) = E(P(X > t \mid Y))$.

Step 4: Operational Rules

1. Linearity: For constants $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$: $$E(aX + bY \mid \mathcal{G}) = a\,E(X \mid \mathcal{G}) + b\,E(Y \mid \mathcal{G})$$ 2. Taking Out What Is Known: If $Z$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable: $$E(Z \cdot X \mid \mathcal{G}) = Z \cdot E(X \mid \mathcal{G})$$ 3. Independence Rule: If $X$ is independent of $\mathcal{G}$: $$E(X \mid \mathcal{G}) = E(X)$$
