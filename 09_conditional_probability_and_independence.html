09 — Conditional Probability & Independence

Measure-theoretic definition of conditioning, law of total probability, and mutual independence.

Step 1: Conditional Probability

For events A, B ∈ ℱ with P(B) ≠ 0:

  P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)

Sample Space Rescaling: Conditioning on B discards all outcomes ω ∉ B (setting their probability to 0) and rescales B to be the new sample space with measure 1.

Step 2: Subsets & Disjoint Special Cases

Subset Ordering: If A ⊆ B, then A ∩ B = A, so:
  • P(A | B) = P(A) / P(B) (not 1!)
  • P(B | A) = P(A) / P(A) = 1 (knowing A happened guarantees B happened).

Disjoint Events: If A ∩ B = ∅, then P(A | B) = 0.

Step 3: Law of Total Probability

Let {B1, B2, …} be a countable partition of Ω (pairwise disjoint, ∪Bi = Ω, P(Bi) ≠ 0).

Slicing A into disjoint components A = ∪(A ∩ Bi) yields:

  P(A) = Σi=1∞ P(A ∩ Bi) = Σi=1∞ P(A | Bi) P(Bi)

Step 4: Pairwise vs. Mutual Independence

Two Events: A, B are independent if P(A ∩ B) = P(A) P(B) ⟺ P(A | B) = P(A).

Mutual Independence: For n events A1, …, An, pairwise independence is insufficient. They must satisfy the product rule for every sub-collection of size k ∈ {2, …, n}:

  P(Ai1 ∩ … ∩ Aik) = P(Ai1) ⋯ P(Aik)

This requires 2n − n − 1 equations in total (e.g., 4 equations for n = 3).
