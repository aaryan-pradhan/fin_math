Martingale, BM and Itô Problems

Elements of Stochastic Modelling, §11.7 Problems 1–27 (assignment on MG, BM, SBM, stochastic calculus and SDE). Each problem is followed by its solution. Sources: Assignments--MG, BM, SBM, Sto. Calculus, SDE (2) (3).pdf (problems) and Solution assignment-- MG, BM, SBM, sto calculus, SDE (2).pdf (solutions). Worked class solutions of Q19–21 are in the first time the BM crosses one of the two.pdf, and of Q24, 26 and 27 in New Note (1).pdf.

Conditional expectation rules used below: CE1 linearity; CE2 pull out what is known: if $Y$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable, $E(YZ\mid\mathcal G)=YE(Z\mid\mathcal G)$; CE3 independence: if $Z\perp\mathcal G$, $E(Z\mid\mathcal G)=EZ$; CE4 tower: $\mathcal H\subset\mathcal G\Rightarrow E(E(Z\mid\mathcal G)\mid\mathcal H)=E(Z\mid\mathcal H)$. Theorem 11.2 is optional stopping, $EX_\tau=EX_0$. Theorem 11.6 gives the three BM martingales $W_t$, $W_t^2-t$ and $e^{uW_t-u^2t/2}$.

Problem 1 — Conditioning a random walk on $S_n$

Problem. Let $X_1,X_2,\dots$ be i.i.d. with $EX_1=\mu$ and $\operatorname{Var}X_1=\sigma^2<\infty$. Put $S_0:=0$ and $S_n:=S_{n-1}+X_n$, $n\ge1$. Compute (i) $E(S_{n+m}\mid S_n)$; (ii) $E(X_1\mid S_n)$, $n\ge1$; (iii) $E(S_{n+m}^2\mid S_n)$; (iv) $E(S_m\mid S_n)$, $m=0,1,\dots,n$.
Hints. (ii) By symmetry, $E(X_1\mid S_n)=E(X_2\mid S_n)$ ($n\ge2$), and so on. (iv) Use one of (i)–(iii).
Solution.
  1. $E(S_{n+m}\mid S_n)\overset{\text{CE1}}=E(S_n\mid S_n)+E(X_{n+1}+\dots+X_{n+m}\mid S_n)\overset{\text{CE2,CE3}}=\mathbf{S_n+m\mu}$.
  2. $S_n=E(X_1+\dots+X_n\mid S_n)=\sum_{k\le n}E(X_k\mid S_n)=nE(X_1\mid S_n)$, so $E(X_1\mid S_n)=\mathbf{S_n/n}$.
  3. $$E(S_{n+m}^2\mid S_n)=S_n^2+2S_nE(X_{n+1}+\dots+X_{n+m})+E(X_{n+1}+\dots+X_{n+m})^2$$ $$=S_n^2+2S_nm\mu+m\sigma^2+m^2\mu^2=\mathbf{(S_n+m\mu)^2+m\sigma^2}.$$
  4. $E(S_m\mid S_n)=\sum_{k\le m}E(X_k\mid S_n)=\mathbf{\tfrac mnS_n}$, by (ii).

Problem 2 — Poisson process conditional moments

Problem. Let $\{N_t\}_{t\ge0}$ be a Poisson process with rate $\lambda>0$ and $\mathcal F_t=\sigma\{N_s,0\le s\le t\}$. Find (i) $E(N_{t+s}\mid\mathcal F_t)$, $s,t\ge0$; (ii) $E(N_{t+s}^2\mid\mathcal F_t)$; (iii) $E(N_s\mid\mathcal F_t)$ and $E(N_s^2\mid\mathcal F_t)$, $0\le s\le t$; (iv) $E(N_s\mid N_t)$ and $E(N_s^2\mid N_t)$, $0\le s\le t$.
Hint. It is not much different from the previous problem.
Solution.
  1. $E(N_{t+s}\mid\mathcal F_t)=N_t+E(N_{t+s}-N_t)=\mathbf{N_t+\lambda s}$ (CE1–CE3).
  2. $\mathbf{(N_t+\lambda s)^2+\lambda s}$, by the argument of Problem 1(iii), using $EN_s=\operatorname{Var}N_s=\lambda s$.
  3. $E(N_s\mid\mathcal F_t)=N_s$ and $E(N_s^2\mid\mathcal F_t)=N_s^2$ by CE2, since $N_s$ (and so $N_s^2$) is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable.
  4. $(N_s\mid N_t=n)\sim\text{Bin}(n,s/t)$ (Example 2.5). So $$E(N_s\mid N_t)=\frac stN_t,\qquad E(N_s^2\mid N_t)=\frac{s(t-s)}{t^2}N_t+\frac{s^2}{t^2}N_t^2 .$$

Problem 3 — Which random times are stopping times?

Problem. Let $\{X_t\}_{t=0,1,\dots,T}$ be a positive process adapted to $\mathbf F=\{\mathcal F_t\}$. In each case, say whether $\tau$ is a stopping time w.r.t. $\mathbf F$. In (iii)–(iv), if the condition is never met for $t\le T$, put $\tau:=T$. Explain, for example by expressing $\{\tau\le t\}$ in terms of the $X_k$. (i) $\tau:=m=$ const; (ii) $\tau:=\tau_1\wedge\tau_2$, where $\tau_1,\tau_2$ are STs; (iii) $\tau:=\min\{t\ge0:X_{t+1}/X_t>1\}$; (iv) $\tau:=\min\{t\ge0:\sum_{k=0}^tX_k>X_t^2\}$; (v) $\tau:=\max\{t\le T:X_t>10\}$.
Solution.
  1. $\{\tau\le t\}=\Omega$ if $m\le t$ and $\emptyset$ if $m>t$. Both are in $\mathcal F_t$. ST.
  2. $\{\tau_1\wedge\tau_2\le t\}=\{\tau_1\le t\}\cup\{\tau_2\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t$. ST. Likewise $\{\tau_1\vee\tau_2\le t\}=\{\tau_1\le t\}\cap\{\tau_2\le t\}$, so the maximum is also an ST.
  3. $\{\tau>t\}=\bigcap_{k=0}^t\{X_{k+1}/X_k\le1\}$. The last event involves $X_{t+1}$, which is in general not $\mathcal F_t$-measurable. Not necessarily an ST (it looks one step ahead).
  4. $\{\tau>t\}=\bigcap_{j=0}^t\{\sum_{k=0}^jX_k\le X_j^2\}\in\mathcal F_t$, so $\{\tau\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t$. ST.
  5. $\{\tau=t\}=\{X_t>10\}\cap\left[\bigcap_{k=t+1}^{T}\{X_k\le10\}\right]$, and the bracket is in general $\notin\mathcal F_t$. Not necessarily an ST (a last-exit time).
The source prints the intersection in (v) as $\bigcap_{k=t+1}^{10}$. The upper index should be $T$: "no later visit above 10 up to the horizon".

Problem 4 — The Lévy martingale

Problem. Let $Y$ be integrable ($E|Y|<\infty$) on a filtered space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbf F=\{\mathcal F_t\}_{t\ge0},\mathbf P)$. Show that $X_t:=E(Y\mid\mathcal F_t)$ is an MG.
Solution.
  • Adapted: by the definition of conditional expectation.
  • Integrable: $E|X_t|\le E|Y|<\infty$ (from CE4).
  • MG property: for $0\le s<t$, by CE4, $E(X_t\mid\mathcal F_s)=E(E(Y\mid\mathcal F_t)\mid\mathcal F_s)=E(Y\mid\mathcal F_s)=X_s$.

Problem 5 — Orthogonal increments

Problem. Let $\{X_t\}_{t\ge0}$ be a square-integrable MG ($EX_t^2<\infty$). Show that it has orthogonal increments: for any $0\le t_1\le t_2\le t_3\le t_4$, $E(X_{t_2}-X_{t_1})(X_{t_4}-X_{t_3})=0$.
Solution. $X_{t_1}$ and $X_{t_2}$ are $\mathcal F_{t_3}$-measurable, so $$E(X_{t_2}-X_{t_1})(X_{t_4}-X_{t_3})=E\left[(X_{t_2}-X_{t_1})\,E(X_{t_4}-X_{t_3}\mid\mathcal F_{t_3})\right]=0,$$ because $E(X_{t_4}-X_{t_3}\mid\mathcal F_{t_3})=X_{t_3}-X_{t_3}=0$. Square integrability makes the product's expectation exist (Cauchy–Bunyakovskii).

Problem 6 — $S_n^2-n\sigma^2$ is a martingale

Problem. Let $S_0:=0$ and $S_n:=Y_1+\dots+Y_n$, with $Y_j$ i.i.d., $EY_j=0$ and $\operatorname{Var}Y_j=\sigma^2<\infty$. Show that $X_n:=S_n^2-n\sigma^2$ is an MG (i) w.r.t. $\mathcal F_n=\sigma(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)$; (ii) w.r.t. its natural filtration $\mathcal F_n'=\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)$.
Hint. (ii) Use (i) and $\mathcal F_n'\subset\mathcal F_n$ (why does this hold?).
Solution. The process is adapted. It is integrable: $E|X_n|\le ES_n^2+n\sigma^2=2n\sigma^2<\infty$.
  1. $$E(X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n)=E(S_n^2+2S_nY_{n+1}+Y_{n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_n)-(n+1)\sigma^2=S_n^2+0+\sigma^2-(n+1)\sigma^2=X_n .$$
  2. Each $X_k=S_k^2-k\sigma^2$, $k\le n$, is a function of $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$, so $\mathcal F_n'\subset\mathcal F_n$. By CE4, $E(X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n')=E[E(X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n)\mid\mathcal F_n']=E(X_n\mid\mathcal F_n')=X_n$.

Problem 7 — Poisson martingales

Problem. Let $\{N_t\}$ be a Poisson process with rate $\lambda>0$. Show that (i) $N_t-\lambda t$; (ii) $(N_t-\lambda t)^2-\lambda t$; (iii) $\exp\{uN_t-\lambda t(e^u-1)\}$ ($u$ a fixed real) are MGs w.r.t. $\mathcal F_t=\sigma(N_s,s\le t)$.
Solution. Same as the proof of Theorem 11.6: write $N_t=N_s+(N_t-N_s)$ with the increment independent of $\mathcal F_s$ and $\sim\text{Poi}(\lambda(t-s))$.
  1. $E(N_t-\lambda t\mid\mathcal F_s)=N_s+\lambda(t-s)-\lambda t=N_s-\lambda s$.
  2. Let $M_t=N_t-\lambda t$ and $D=M_t-M_s$, so $ED=0$ and $ED^2=\lambda(t-s)$. Then $E(M_t^2\mid\mathcal F_s)=M_s^2+2M_s\cdot0+\lambda(t-s)$, so $E(M_t^2-\lambda t\mid\mathcal F_s)=M_s^2-\lambda s$.
  3. The Poisson MGF is $Ee^{u(N_t-N_s)}=e^{\lambda(t-s)(e^u-1)}$. So $E(e^{uN_t}\mid\mathcal F_s)=e^{uN_s}e^{\lambda(t-s)(e^u-1)}$, and multiplying by $e^{-\lambda t(e^u-1)}$ gives $e^{uN_s-\lambda s(e^u-1)}$.

Problem 8 — Symmetric walk between two barriers

Problem. Let $S_0:=0$ and $S_n:=Y_1+\dots+Y_n$, with $Y_j$ i.i.d., $P(Y_1=1)=P(Y_1=-1)=\tfrac12$. Let $\tau:=\min\{n\ge0:S_n=a\text{ or }S_n=b\}$ for integers $a<0<b$. Use Theorem 11.2, without checking its conditions in detail, to (i) find the distribution of $S_\tau$; (ii) compute $E\tau$.
Hints. (i) Use the MG $X_n:=S_n$. (ii) Use (i) and the MG from Problem 6.
Solution.
  1. $0=ES_0=ES_\tau=aP(S_\tau=a)+bP(S_\tau=b)=(a-b)P(S_\tau=a)+b$, so $$P(S_\tau=a)=\frac b{b-a},\qquad P(S_\tau=b)=\frac{-a}{b-a}.$$
  2. Apply Theorem 11.2 to $X_n=S_n^2-n$: $0=E(S_\tau^2-\tau)=\dfrac{a^2b}{b-a}+\dfrac{b^2(-a)}{b-a}-E\tau=-ab-E\tau$. So $E\tau=\mathbf{-ab}$.

Problem 9 — Martingale transforms and the doubling strategy

Problem. In a fair game betting \$1 per play (win/lose w.p. $\tfrac12$, independently), $\{X_n:=$ fortune after $n$ plays$\}$ is an MG w.r.t. its history $\mathbf F$. Now let the stake on play $n$ be a bounded amount $Y_n$ chosen before the play, from the history up to play $n-1$. So $|Y_n|\le C_n<\infty$ and $\{Y_n\}$ is predictable. The net gain after $n$ plays is $$Z_n:=\sum_{k=1}^nY_k(X_k-X_{k-1}),\qquad Z_0=0,$$ the martingale transform of $X_n$ by $Y_n$.
  1. Show that $\{Z_n\}$ is an MG w.r.t. $\mathbf F$.
  2. The "martingale" strategy: start with a unit stake and double it after each loss; reset to one after each win. Represent the net gain $\{Z_n\}$ as a martingale transform by specifying $\{X_n\}$ and $\{Y_n\}$.
  3. Stop at the time $\tau$ of the first win. Find the distribution and expectation of $\tau$, and check whether Theorem 11.2 holds for $\{Z_n\}$ and $\tau$.
  4. Compute $E(Z_n;\tau>n)$ and check whether condition (11.11), sufficient for (11.9), holds.
Solution.
  1. Adapted. Integrable: $|Z_n|\le\max_{j\le n}|X_j-X_{j-1}|\sum_{k\le n}|Y_k|\le\sum_{k\le n}C_k$. MG: $E(Z_{n+1}-Z_n\mid\mathcal F_n)=Y_{n+1}E(X_{n+1}-X_n\mid\mathcal F_n)=0$ by CE2.
  2. $X_n$ is the \$1-per-play fortune. $Y_1=1$, and for $n>1$, $Y_n=2Y_{n-1}$ if $X_{n-1}-X_{n-2}=-1$ (loss) and $Y_n=1$ if it was $+1$ (win).
  3. With $\xi_n:=X_n-X_{n-1}$ i.i.d. $\pm1$: $$P(\tau=n)=P(\xi_1=\dots=\xi_{n-1}=-1,\ \xi_n=1)=2^{-n},\quad n\ge1,$$ a geometric distribution. $E\tau=\sum n2^{-n}=2$ (or via the GF $g_\tau(z)=z/(2-z)$, $g'_\tau(1)=2$). Theorem 11.2 fails: at the first win you always have $Z_\tau=1$, so $EZ_\tau=1\ne0=Z_0$.
  4. $E(Z_n;\tau>n)=E(Z_n;\xi_1=\dots=\xi_n=-1)=-\sum_{j=1}^n2^{j-1}\cdot2^{-n}=-(2^n-1)2^{-n}=2^{-n}-1\to-1\neq0$. Condition (11.11) is not satisfied.

Problem 10 — Up 1 / down 2 walk

Problem. $S_0=0$, and each step goes up 1 w.p. $p=\tfrac67$ and down 2 w.p. $\tfrac17$. (i) Show that $X_n:=2^{-S_n}$ is an MG. (ii) Let $\tau:=\min\{n\ge0:X_n\le0.1\}$. Use Theorem 11.2 (without verifying its conditions) and the MG $Z_n:=S_n-ES_n$ to compute $E\tau$.
Hint. Express $\tau$ through $S_n$. What values can $S_\tau$ take? If $S_n>S_{n-1}$, then $S_n=S_{n-1}+1$.
Solution.
  1. Use the natural filtration. Integrable since $0<X_n\le2^{2n}$. With $Y_n:=S_n-S_{n-1}$: $$E(X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n)=X_nE2^{-Y_{n+1}}=X_n\left(2^{-1}\cdot\tfrac67+2^2\cdot\tfrac17\right)=X_n .$$
  2. $\{X_n\le0.1\}=\{S_n\ge\log_210\}=\{S_n\ge4\}$ since $S_n$ is integer. So $\tau=\min\{n:S_n=4\}$: the walk moves up one step at a time, so it cannot jump over level 4, and it hits 4 w.p. 1 because $EY_1=\tfrac67-\tfrac27=\tfrac47>0$. Then $$0=EZ_\tau=E(S_\tau-\tfrac47\tau)=4-\tfrac47E\tau\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E\tau=\mathbf 7.$$

Problem 11 — Asymmetric simple walk

Problem. A simple walk goes up 1 w.p. $p\in(0,1)$ and down 1 w.p. $q=1-p$, with $p\ne\tfrac12$. (i) Show that $X_n:=(q/p)^{S_n}$ is an MG. (ii) With $S_0=0$ and $\tau:=\min\{n\ge0:S_n=a\text{ or }S_n=b\}$, $a<0<b$ integers, use Theorem 11.2 and (i) to find the distribution of $S_\tau$. Then use Theorem 11.2 and $Z_n:=S_n-n(p-q)$ to compute $E\tau$.
Solution.
  1. Natural filtration; bounded on each $n$. $E(X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n)=X_nE(q/p)^{Y_{n+1}}=X_n\left((q/p)p+(q/p)^{-1}q\right)=X_n$.
  2. Let $r:=q/p$. $1=EX_\tau=r^aP(S_\tau=a)+r^bP(S_\tau=b)$ gives $$P(S_\tau=a)=\frac{r^b-1}{r^b-r^a},\qquad P(S_\tau=b)=1-P(S_\tau=a).$$ From $0=EZ_\tau=ES_\tau-(2p-1)E\tau$: $$E\tau=\frac{b(1-r^a)+a(r^b-1)}{(2p-1)(r^b-r^a)} .$$

Problem 12 — Uniform samples are dense

Problem. Show that $\{U_1,U_2,\dots\}$, where the $U_j$ are i.i.d. $U(0,1)$, is dense in $[0,1]$ with probability 1.
Hint. Use the Glivenko–Cantelli theorem (2.87).
Solution. Suppose that for some $\omega$ the set is not dense. Then there are $t_1<t_2$ in $[0,1]$ with $U_j(\omega)\notin(t_1,t_2)$ for all $j$, so the EDFs satisfy $F_n^*(t_1)=F_n^*(t_2)$ for every $n$. This contradicts (2.87), since the limit $F(t)=t$ has $F(t_1)\ne F(t_2)$. So such $\omega$ form a null set.

Problem 13 — Distribution of $2W_{t_1}-W_{t_2}$

Problem. Find the distribution of $X:=2W_{t_1}-W_{t_2}$, $0<t_1<t_2$.
Solution. $X$ is normal, as a linear transform of a Gaussian vector. $EX=0$ and $$\operatorname{Var}X=4EW_{t_1}^2-4EW_{t_1}W_{t_2}+EW_{t_2}^2=4t_1-4t_1+t_2=t_2 .$$ So $X\sim\mathbf{N(0,t_2)}$. Alternatively, $X=W_{t_1}-(W_{t_2}-W_{t_1})$ is a sum of independent $N(0,t_1)$ and $N(0,t_2-t_1)$ terms.

Problem 14 — Distribution of $W_0+W_2-W_3+2W_4$

Problem. Find the distribution of $X:=W_0+W_2-W_3+2W_4$.
Solution. Normal with mean 0. By independent increments, $$X=2W_2+(W_3-W_2)+2(W_4-W_3)\sim N(0,\,4\cdot2+1+4\cdot1)=\mathbf{N(0,13)}.$$

Problem 15 — BM finite-dimensional density

Problem. Derive the BM's FDD density $f_{t_1,\dots,t_k}(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ (see (11.22)) using (2.33). Use the fact that $(W_{t_1},\dots,W_{t_n})$ is a simple linear transformation of $(W_{t_1}-W_{t_0},\dots,W_{t_n}-W_{t_{n-1}})$, whose components are independent, so that the latter vector's density is just the product of the increment densities.
Solution. In the notation of (2.33), $\mathbf X=(W_{t_1}-W_{t_0},\dots,W_{t_n}-W_{t_{n-1}})$, $\mathbf Y=(W_{t_1},\dots,W_{t_n})$ and $g(\mathbf x)=\mathbf xA$, with $$A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\0&1&\cdots&1\\\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\0&0&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}.$$ $g^{-1}$ is linear with $|J|=|\det A|^{-1}=1$, so $$f_{\mathbf Y}(\mathbf y)=f_{\mathbf X}(g^{-1}(\mathbf y))=\prod_{j=1}^nf_{W_{t_j}-W_{t_{j-1}}}(x_j-x_{j-1}),$$ which is (11.22).

Problem 16 — Matrix form of BM simulation

Problem. The map from a standard normal $\mathbf Z\in\mathbb R^n$ to the BM values in the simulation algorithm on p. 320 can be written as $(W_{t_1},\dots,W_{t_n})=\mathbf ZA$, $A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$. Specify $A$. How does the representation change if you simulate $(X_{t_1},\dots,X_{t_n})$ for the arithmetic BM (11.16) directly?
Solution. As in Problem 15, but with the increments scaled: $$A=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{t_1-t_0}&\sqrt{t_1-t_0}&\cdots&\sqrt{t_1-t_0}\\0&\sqrt{t_2-t_1}&\cdots&\sqrt{t_2-t_1}\\\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\0&0&\cdots&\sqrt{t_n-t_{n-1}}\end{pmatrix}.$$ For the arithmetic BM, multiply $A$ by $\sigma$ and add the drift vector $(\mu t_1,\dots,\mu t_n)$.

Problem 17 — Joint densities

Problem. Compute the joint densities of (i) $(2W_3,W_5)$ and (ii) $(W_2,2W_3,W_5)$.
Solution.
  1. $2W_3\sim N(0,12)$ and, by independent increments, $(W_5\mid2W_3=x)\sim N(x/2,2)$. By (2.80), $$f_{(2W_3,W_5)}(x,y)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot2}}e^{-(y-x/2)^2/4}\cdot\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot12}}e^{-x^2/24}=\frac1{4\pi\sqrt6}\exp\left\{-\frac{y^2}4+\frac{xy}4-\frac{5x^2}{48}\right\}.$$
  2. In the same way, $f_{(W_2,2W_3)}(x,y)=\dfrac1{4\pi\sqrt2}\exp\{-\tfrac{y^2}8+\tfrac{xy}2-\tfrac{3x^2}4\}$. By independent increments, $(W_5\mid W_2=x,2W_3=y)\sim N(y/2,2)$, so $$f_{(W_2,2W_3,W_5)}(x,y,z)=\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{-(z-y/2)^2/4}f_{(W_2,2W_3)}(x,y)=\frac1{8\sqrt2\,\pi^{3/2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{3x^2}4+\frac{xy}2-\frac{3y^2}{16}+\frac{yz}4-\frac{z^2}4\right\}.$$

Problem 18 — Time inversion $tW_{1/t}$

Problem. Use Theorem 11.4 to show that $\widetilde W_t:=tW_{1/t}$ is a standard BM, given that $W_t$ is.
Solution. $E\widetilde W_t=0$. For $0<s<t$: $E\widetilde W_s\widetilde W_t=st\left(\tfrac1s\wedge\tfrac1t\right)=st\cdot\tfrac1t=s$. The process is Gaussian: $(\widetilde W_{t_1},\dots,\widetilde W_{t_n})$ is a linear transform of the Gaussian vector $(W_{1/t_1},\dots,W_{1/t_n})$. Theorem 11.4 gives the result.

Problem 19 — Exit through parabolic boundaries

Problem. Let $\tau:=\min\{t>0:W_t=\pm\sqrt{a+bt}\}$ be the first time the BM crosses one of the two parabolic boundaries $\pm\sqrt{a+bt}$, where $a>0$ and $b\in(0,1)$. Use Theorems 11.6 and 11.2 to compute $E\tau$.
Solution. Use the MG $Y_t:=W_t^2-t$. At $\tau$, $W_\tau^2=a+b\tau$, so $$0=EY_0=EY_\tau=E(W_\tau^2-\tau)=E(a+b\tau-\tau)=a-(1-b)E\tau\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E\tau=\frac a{1-b}.$$

Problem 20 — Crossing the line $2t-4$

Problem. Let $\tau:=\min\{t>0:W_t\le2t-4\}$ be the first time the BM crosses the boundary $v_t:=2t-4$. Using the three BM martingales (Theorem 11.6) and Theorem 11.2 (without verifying its conditions), compute (i) $E\tau$; (ii) $\operatorname{Var}(\tau)$; (iii) the Laplace transform $l_\tau(s)=Ee^{-s\tau}$, $s\ge0$; (iv) $EW_\tau$ and $EW_\tau^2$.
Solution. At $\tau$, $W_\tau=2\tau-4$.
  1. With $W_t$: $0=EW_\tau=E(2\tau-4)$, so $E\tau=\mathbf2$.
  2. With $W_t^2-t$: $0=E((2\tau-4)^2-\tau)=4E\tau^2-17E\tau+16=4E\tau^2-18$, so $E\tau^2=4.5$ and $\operatorname{Var}\tau=4.5-4=\mathbf{0.5}$.
  3. With $Z_t:=e^{uW_t-u^2t/2}$: $$1=Ee^{u(2\tau-4)-u^2\tau/2}=e^{-4u}Ee^{-(u^2/2-2u)\tau}=e^{-4u}Ee^{-s\tau},\qquad s:=\tfrac{u^2}2-2u .$$ Solving, $u=2\pm\sqrt{2(s+2)}$. Take "$-$": we need $l_\tau(s)\le1$ for $s\ge0$, i.e. $u\le0$. So $$l_\tau(s)=\exp\{8-4\sqrt{2(s+2)}\}.$$
  4. By Theorem 11.2: $EW_\tau=\mathbf0$, and $EW_\tau^2=E\tau=\mathbf2$.

Problem 21 — Exit from $(a,b)$

Problem. Let $\tau:=\min\{t>0:W_t=a\text{ or }W_t=b\}$, $a<0<b$. Using the three BM martingales and Theorem 11.2 (without verifying its conditions): (i) find the distribution of $W_\tau$; (ii) compute $E\tau$; (iii) compute $l_\tau(s)=Ee^{-s\tau}$, $s\ge0$, when $a=-1$, $b=1$; (iv) use (iii) to compute $E\tau$ when $a=-1$, $b=1$, and compare with (ii); (v) use (iii) to compute $\operatorname{Var}(\tau)$ when $a=-1$, $b=1$.
Solution.
  1. $0=EW_\tau=aP(W_\tau=a)+b(1-P(W_\tau=a))$, so $P(W_\tau=a)=\dfrac b{b-a}$ and $P(W_\tau=b)=\dfrac{-a}{b-a}$.
  2. $E\tau=EW_\tau^2=a^2\dfrac b{b-a}+b^2\dfrac{-a}{b-a}=\mathbf{-ab}$ (see also (12.31)).
  3. Take $b=-a$ for this part. $1=EZ_\tau=E\left(e^{-u^2\tau/2}E(e^{uW_\tau}\mid\tau)\right)$. By symmetry $W_\tau=\pm a$ with equal probabilities, independently of $\tau$, so $1=\tfrac12(e^{au}+e^{-au})Ee^{-u^2\tau/2}$. With $s=u^2/2$: $$l_\tau(s)=\frac1{\cosh(\sqrt{2s}\,a)}\ \overset{a=1}=\ \frac1{\cosh\sqrt{2s}}.$$
  4. $-l_\tau'(s)=\dfrac{\sinh(\sqrt{2s}a)}{\cosh^2(\sqrt{2s}a)}\dfrac a{\sqrt{2s}}\to a^2$ as $s\to0$. So $E\tau=a^2=1$, which agrees with (ii) at $a=-b$.
  5. With $x^2=2s$, $\cosh x=1+s+\tfrac{s^2}6+\dots$, so $l_\tau(s)=1-s+\tfrac56s^2-\dots$. Hence $E\tau^2=2\cdot\tfrac56=\tfrac53$ and $\operatorname{Var}\tau=\tfrac53-1=\mathbf{\tfrac23}$.
The source leaves (v) as "DIY. Just keep differentiating"; the value $2/3$ above is worked out here.

Problem 22 — Itô isometry for simple processes

Problem. Let $f_t$ and $g_t$ be simple processes on $[0,T]$ on a common filtered probability space with a BM $\{W_t\}$. Show by direct calculation that $EI_t(f)I_t(g)=\int_0^tEf_sg_s\,ds$, $t\in[0,T]$.
Solution. Without loss of generality both use a common partition $0=t_0<\dots<t_n=T$: $f_t=X_k$ and $g_t=Y_k$ on $[t_{k-1},t_k)$, with $X_k,Y_k$ being $\mathcal F_{t_{k-1}}$-measurable. Then $$EI_t(f)I_t(g)=\sum_{k}\underbrace{E\,X_kY_k(W_{t_k}-W_{t_{k-1}})^2}_{A_k}+\sum_{j\ne k}\underbrace{E\,X_kY_j(W_{t_k}-W_{t_{k-1}})(W_{t_j}-W_{t_{j-1}})}_{B_{jk}} .$$ By CE4, $A_k=E\left(X_kY_kE((W_{t_k}-W_{t_{k-1}})^2\mid\mathcal F_{t_{k-1}})\right)=(t_k-t_{k-1})EX_kY_k$. For $j<k$, $B_{jk}=E\left(X_kY_j(W_{t_j}-W_{t_{j-1}})E(W_{t_k}-W_{t_{k-1}}\mid\mathcal F_{t_{k-1}})\right)=0$. So $EI_t(f)I_t(g)=\sum_k(t_k-t_{k-1})EX_kY_k=\int_0^tEf_sg_s\,ds$.

This is also an immediate consequence of the Itô isometry (Theorem 11.7(iii)). A linear map that preserves norms also preserves inner products: $\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2+2(u,v)=\|u+v\|^2=\|L(u+v)\|^2=\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2+2(Lu,Lv)$.

Problem 23 — The exponential martingale

Problem. Let $g_t$ be non-random on $[0,T]$ with $\int_0^Tg_t^2dt<\infty$. Use Itô's formula to show that $$Y_t=\exp\left\{\int_0^tg_s\,dW_s-\frac12\int_0^tg_s^2\,ds\right\},\qquad t\in[0,T],$$ is an MG.
Solution. $Y_t=f(X_t)$ with $f(x)=e^x$ ($f'=f''=f$) and $dX_t=g_tdW_t-\tfrac12g_t^2dt$. By Itô, $$dY_t=f(X_t)\left(g_tdW_t-\tfrac12g_t^2dt\right)+\tfrac12f(X_t)g_t^2dt=Y_tg_t\,dW_t .$$ There is no $dt$ term, so $Y$ is an MG (Remark 11.3).

Problem 24 — $d\cos(W_t)$

Problem. Compute the stochastic differential $d\cos(W_t)$.
Solution. $f(x)=\cos x$, $f'=-\sin x$, $f''=-\cos x$, and $(dW_t)^2=dt$: $$d\cos W_t=-\sin(W_t)\,dW_t-\tfrac12\cos(W_t)\,dt .$$

Problem 25 — $e^{-2X_t}$ with $X_t=t+W_t$

Problem. Put $X_t:=t+W_t$. (i) Use Itô's formula to compute $de^{-2X_t}$. (ii) Is $Y_t:=e^{-2X_t}$ a martingale? Explain.
Solution.
  1. $f(x)=e^{-2x}$, $f'=-2f$, $f''=4f$, $dX_t=dt+dW_t$, $(dX_t)^2=dt$: $$df(X_t)=-2f(X_t)(dt+dW_t)+\tfrac12\cdot4f(X_t)\,dt=-2f(X_t)\,dW_t .$$
  2. Yes: it has no drift (Remark 11.3).

Problem 26 — Solving a GBM SDE

Problem. A risky asset follows $dS_t=0.2S_tdt+S_tdW_t$, $t\ge0$, $S_0=5$ (a special case of the Black–Scholes framework, Chapter 13). (i) It is suspected that the solution has the form $S_t=ce^{at+bW_t}$ for constants $a,b,c$. Use Itô's formula to verify this and find $a$, $b$, $c$. (ii) Show that $X_t=1/S_t$ satisfies $dX_t=0.8X_tdt-X_tdW_t$, $X_0=0.2$.
Solution.
  1. $f(t,x)=ce^{at+bx}$: $\partial_tf=af$, $\partial_xf=bf$, $\partial_{xx}f=b^2f$. So $$dS_t=\left(a+\tfrac{b^2}2\right)S_tdt+bS_tdW_t .$$ Matching: $b=1$ and $a+\tfrac12=0.2$, so $a=-0.3$. $S_0=ce^0=5$ gives $c=5$. So $S_t=5e^{-0.3t+W_t}$.
  2. $X_t=0.2e^{0.3t-W_t}=f(t,W_t)$ with $\partial_tf=0.3f$, $\partial_xf=-f$, $\partial_{xx}f=f$: $$dX_t=0.3X_tdt-X_tdW_t+\tfrac12X_tdt=0.8X_tdt-X_tdW_t,\qquad X_0=0.2 .$$ (Or apply Itô to $f(S)=1/S$: $-S^{-2}(0.2Sdt+SdW)+S^{-3}S^2dt=0.8X\,dt-X\,dW$.)

Problem 27 — Heston variance → volatility SDE

Problem. The Heston stochastic-volatility model assumes the variance process follows $dV_t=(1-V_t)dt+2\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t$, $V_0=1$. (i) Derive an SDE for the volatility process $Z_t=\sqrt{V_t}$ and find its initial condition $Z_0$. (ii) Show that $Z_t:=e^{-t/2}\left(1+\int_0^te^{s/2}dW_s\right)$ satisfies that SDE and initial condition.
Solution.
  1. $f(x)=\sqrt x$, $f'=\tfrac12x^{-1/2}$, $f''=-\tfrac14x^{-3/2}$, and $(dV_t)^2=4V_tdt$: $$dZ_t=\tfrac12V_t^{-1/2}\left((1-V_t)dt+2\sqrt{V_t}dW_t\right)-\tfrac18V_t^{-3/2}\cdot4V_tdt=-\tfrac12Z_tdt+dW_t .$$ This is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck SDE ((11.46) with $\alpha=\sigma=1$), with $Z_0=\sqrt{V_0}=1$.
  2. $Z_t=X_tY_t$ with $X_t=e^{-t/2}$ ($dX_t=-\tfrac12X_tdt$) and $Y_t=1+\int_0^te^{s/2}dW_s$ ($dY_t=e^{t/2}dW_t$). Since $dX_tdY_t=0$, the product rule (Theorem 11.10) gives $$dZ_t=Y_tdX_t+X_tdY_t=-\tfrac12Z_tdt+dW_t,\qquad Z_0=e^0(1+0)=1 .$$
