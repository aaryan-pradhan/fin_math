# p.143–145: The Black–Scholes framework

## The bond and the stock (p.143)

<!-- anim: gbm_paths S0=100 mu=0.1 sigma=0.2 T=1 say="Six possible paths of a stock with $\mu=0.1$, $\sigma=0.2$. On average it grows like $S_0e^{\mu t}$." -->

Now time is continuous. The bond is

$$B_t = e^{rt}, \quad t \in [0, T]$$

Equivalently, $dB_t = rB_t\,dt$ with $B_0 = 1$.

There is one risky asset, a stock. Its price $S_t$ satisfies the Black–Scholes SDE:

$$dS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t, \quad t \in [0, T] \qquad (\star)$$

Here $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$, $\sigma > 0$, $S_0 > 0$ is a constant, and $\{W_t\}$ is a standard BM (Wiener process).

Divide by $S_t$:

$$\frac{dS_t}{S_t} = \mu\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t \qquad (1)$$

In words: the return over a short time is a steady drift $\mu\,dt$ plus a random kick $\sigma\,dW_t$.

Numbers. With $\mu = 0.1$ and $\sigma = 0.2$, over $dt = 0.01$ the drift adds $0.1\%$, and the kick has standard deviation $0.2 \times \sqrt{0.01} = 2\%$.

## Solving the SDE with Itô's formula (p.143–144)

Apply Itô's formula to $\ln S_t$. Its first derivative is $\frac{1}{S}$ and its second is $-\frac{1}{S^2}$:

$$d(\ln S_t) = \frac{1}{S_t}\,dS_t - \frac{1}{2S_t^2}(dS_t)^2$$

From $(\star)$, $(dS_t)^2 = \sigma^2 S_t^2\,dt$, since $(dW_t)^2 = dt$ and the other products are $0$:

$$d(\ln S_t) = \frac{dS_t}{S_t} - \frac{1}{2S_t^2} \times \sigma^2 S_t^2\,dt$$

Substitute (1) for $\frac{dS_t}{S_t}$:

$$d(\ln S_t) = \mu\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\,dt = \left(\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)dt + \sigma\,dW_t$$

Integrate from $0$ to $t$, using $W_0 = 0$:

$$\ln S_t - \ln S_0 = \left(\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t + \sigma W_t$$

Take $e$ to both sides:

$$S_t = S_0\, e^{\left(\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t + \sigma W_t}, \quad t \in [0, T] \qquad (\star_1)$$

So the stock is a geometric BM. On p.133 the notes wrote $S_0e^{\mu t + \sigma W_t}$; that $\mu$ is this $\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$.

Numbers. $S_0 = 100$, $\mu = 0.1$, $\sigma = 0.2$, $t = 1$, and say $W_1 = 0$: $S_1 = 100\,e^{0.1 - 0.02} = 100\,e^{0.08} = 108.33$.

## Two questions (p.144)

Q1. Is the Black–Scholes market arbitrage-free? If yes, how do we price attainable claims?

Q2. Is the Black–Scholes market complete?

The answers to Q1 and Q2 are given in terms of EMMs on $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$: which probability measures, equivalent to $P$, make the discounted price $e^{-rt}S_t$ a MG?

## A family of candidate prices (p.144)

Let

$$Z_t := S_0 \exp\left\{\int_0^t a_s\,ds + \sigma W_t\right\}, \quad t \in [0, T]$$

where $\{a_t\}_{t \in [0,T]}$ is an adapted process on $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{F}, P)$.

In words: $Z_t$ looks like $(\star_1)$, but the drift is left free. With $a_t = \mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$ it is exactly $S_t$.

Consider $\{e^{-rt}Z_t\}$. It is an Itô process, and it will be a MG iff the coefficient of the $dt$-term in its stochastic differential is zero.

The fact used here (boxed in the notes): an Itô process

$$X_t = X_0 + \int_0^t a_s\,ds + \int_0^t b_s\,dW_s, \quad t \in [0, T]$$

is a MG iff $a_t = 0$ for all $t \in [0, T]$.

Numbers. For $Z_t$ itself the drift $a_t$ is a constant like $0.03$ or $0.08$. Only one value will make $e^{-rt}Z_t$ a MG.

## The drift that makes it a martingale (p.145)

Split $e^{-rt}Z_t$ into two factors:

$$e^{-rt}Z_t = \underbrace{\exp\left\{\int_0^t a_s\,ds\right\}}_{=: X_t} \times \underbrace{S_0\exp\{-rt + \sigma W_t\}}_{=: Y_t}$$

The notes leave out the constant $S_0$; it only scales everything, so nothing changes.

Use the product rule for Itô processes:

$$d(e^{-rt}Z_t) = Y_t\,dX_t + X_t\,dY_t + dX_t\,dY_t$$

$X_t$ has no $dW$ part, so the ordinary chain rule gives $dX_t = a_tX_t\,dt$. Itô's formula on $e^{-rt + \sigma w}$ gives $dY_t = -rY_t\,dt + \sigma Y_t\,dW_t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 Y_t\,dt$. And $dX_t\,dY_t = 0$, because $dt \cdot dt = dt \cdot dW_t = 0$:

$$d(e^{-rt}Z_t) = Y_t a_t X_t\,dt + X_t\left(-rY_t\,dt + \sigma Y_t\,dW_t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 Y_t\,dt\right) + 0$$

Collect the $dt$ terms:

$$d(e^{-rt}Z_t) = X_tY_t\left(a_t - r + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)dt + \sigma X_tY_t\,dW_t$$

As $X_tY_t > 0$, the coefficient of $dt$ is zero iff

$$a_t = r - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}, \quad t \in [0, T]$$

In words: the stock has drift $\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$ under $P$. Under the EMM it has drift $r - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$: the $\mu$ is replaced by $r$.

Numbers. $r = 0.05$, $\sigma = 0.2$: $a_t = 0.05 - 0.02 = 0.03$. Check the $dt$ coefficient: $0.03 - 0.05 + 0.02 = 0$.

## The answer (p.145)

According to the previous section (p.142–143), the Black–Scholes market is arbitrage-free and complete. So one can price any contingent claim $X$ in the market using the valuation formula

$$P_t(X) = E^*\left(e^{-r(T-t)}X \mid \mathcal{F}_t\right)$$

Numbers. For the call with $S_0 = K = 100$, $r = 0.05$, $\sigma = 0.2$, $T = 1$, this $E^*$ at $t = 0$ is the Black–Scholes price $10.45$ from p.133.
