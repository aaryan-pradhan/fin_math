# p.142–143: Martingales and claim pricing

## The setting (p.142)

The price process is

$$\{S_t := (S_t^1, \dots, S_t^n)\}_{t \ge 0}$$

It is a stochastic process on $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$, with $n$ different risky assets.

There is also a bond, or bank account:

$$B_t = (1+r)^t \text{ in discrete time } t = 0, 1, 2, \dots, \qquad B_t = e^{rt} \text{ in continuous time } t \ge 0$$

with $r \ge 0$.

A European contingent claim $X \ge 0$ has maturity $T$. Usually it is a function of the prices, $X = g(S_t)$ with $t \le T$.

Numbers. A call on one stock with $K = 4$ and $T = 2$ is $X = g(S_2) = (S_2 - 4)^+$.

## The fair price (p.142)

The "risk neutral" or "arbitrage free" price of a claim is the value of the self-financing portfolio that replicates the claim $X$ at the maturity time $T$.

This is exactly what we did on the binomial tree (p.125–129). Here are the three key facts of the no-arbitrage pricing theory.

## Fact 1: an EMM means no arbitrage (p.142)

Suppose there is an EMM $P^*$ on $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$, and the discounted price process

$$S_t^* = (1+r)^{-t} S_t \text{ in discrete time}, \qquad S_t^* = e^{-rt} S_t \text{ in continuous time}$$

is a MG on $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P^*)$. Then the market is arbitrage free.

In words: if some probabilities make every discounted stock a fair game, free money is impossible. That is Thm 2 (p.107), now for any number of periods.

Numbers. On our tree, $p^* = 0.5$ and $E^*(S_1^*) = \frac{0.5 \times 8 + 0.5 \times 2}{1.25} = 4 = S_0$. So that market is arbitrage free.

## Fact 2: the price of an attainable claim (p.143)

<!-- anim: backward_values T=2 S0=4 u=2 d=0.5 r=0.25 K=4 say="On the tree: the call's price at each node is the discounted $p^*$-average of the next values." -->

For an attainable claim $X$ with maturity $T$, its arbitrage-free price at time $t$ is

$$P_t(X) = E^*\left((1+r)^{t-T} X \mid \mathcal{F}_t\right) \text{ in discrete time}$$

$$P_t(X) = E^*\left(e^{-r(T-t)} X \mid \mathcal{F}_t\right) \text{ in continuous time}, \quad t \in [0, T]$$

In words: average the payoff under $P^*$, given everything known at time $t$, and discount it over the time left, $T - t$.

At $t = 0$ nothing is known yet, so the conditioning drops:

$$P_0(X) = E^*\left((1+r)^{-T} X\right) \quad \text{or} \quad E^*\left(e^{-rT} X\right)$$

Numbers. On the tree, $P_0(X) = \frac{0.25 \times 12}{1.25^2} = 1.92$, and at the node $S_1 = 8$, $P_1(X) = \frac{0.5 \times 12}{1.25} = 4.8$.

## Fact 3: a unique EMM means a complete market (p.143)

If the EMM $P^*$ is unique, then the market is complete. That is, any claim is attainable, and so it can always be priced.

This is Thm 4 (p.111), stated for any market.

Numbers. On the binomial tree $p^* = \frac{1+r-d}{u-d} = 0.5$ is the only choice, so every claim on that tree has one fair price.
