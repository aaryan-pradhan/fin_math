Additive & Geometric Random Walks

Rigorously deriving the martingale conditions for discrete additive random walks and exponential (geometric) random walks.

Step 1: Additive Random Walk

Definition: Let $(Y_j)_{j \geq 1}$ be i.i.d. random variables with $E[|Y_1|] < \infty$. Define $X_0 = 0$ and $X_n = \sum_{j=1}^{n} Y_j$ for $n \geq 1$, with natural filtration $\mathcal{F}_n = \sigma(Y_1, \ldots, Y_n)$.
Derivation of $E[X_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_n]$:
  1. Split: $X_{n+1} = X_n + Y_{n+1}$.
  2. Linearity: $E[X_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_n] = E[X_n \mid \mathcal{F}_n] + E[Y_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_n]$.
  3. Measurability & Independence: Since $X_n$ is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable and $Y_{n+1}$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_n$:
$$E[X_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_n] = X_n + E[Y_1]$$ Conclusion:
  • $(X_n)$ is a martingale $\iff E[Y_1] = 0$.
  • $(X_n)$ is a submartingale $\iff E[Y_1] \geq 0$.
  • $(X_n)$ is a supermartingale $\iff E[Y_1] \leq 0$.

Step 2: Geometric (Exponential) Random Walk

Definition: Let $(Y_j)_{j \geq 1}$ be i.i.d. random variables and $X_0 > 0$ constant. Define the geometric random walk: $$X_n = X_0 e^{Y_1 + \cdots + Y_n} = X_{n-1} e^{Y_n} \quad (n \geq 1)$$ with natural filtration $\mathcal{F}_n = \sigma(Y_1, \ldots, Y_n)$.
Derivation of $E[X_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_n]$:
  1. Product Form: $X_{n+1} = X_n \cdot e^{Y_{n+1}}$.
  2. Taking Out What Is Known: Since $X_n$ is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable: $E[X_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_n] = X_n \cdot E[e^{Y_{n+1}} \mid \mathcal{F}_n]$.
  3. Independence Rule: Since $Y_{n+1}$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_n$:
$$E[X_{n+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_n] = X_n \cdot E[e^{Y_1}] = X_n \cdot \phi_Y(1)$$ Conclusion: $(X_n)$ is a martingale $\iff E[e^{Y_1}] = 1$ (i.e. $\phi_Y(1) = 1$).

Step 3: Normal Log-Increments Case

Special Case (Log-Normal): If $Y_j \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ i.i.d., then its Moment Generating Function is: $$E[e^{Y_1}] = e^{\mu + \frac12 \sigma^2}$$ For $X_n = X_0 e^{\sum Y_j}$ to be a martingale: $$e^{\mu + \frac12 \sigma^2} = 1 \implies \mu = -\tfrac12 \sigma^2$$
This exact drift correction ($\mu = -\tfrac12\sigma^2$) appears fundamentally in continuous-time geometric Brownian motion and Black-Scholes asset modeling.
