Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM) & Filtration Expectation

Log-Normal Stock Modeling, Conditional Expectations, and Martingale Drift Correction.

1. Definition of Geometric Brownian Motion

Let $Y(t)$ be Brownian motion with drift $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$: $Y(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu t, \sigma^2 t)$.
The exponentiated process: $$X(t) = e^{Y(t)}$$ is called Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM).
  • Strictly positive: $X(t) > 0$ for all $t$.
  • Log-Normal distribution: $\log X(t) = Y(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu t, \sigma^2 t)$.

2. Grounding Filtrations $\mathcal{F}_s$: Discrete vs Continuous

Discrete 3-Coin-Flip Example: If wealth $X_3 = Y_1 + Y_2 + Y_3$ where $Y_i = \pm 1$ with prob $1/2$, the filtration $\mathcal{F}_1$ is the observed outcome of flip 1.
$$\mathbb{E}[X_3 \mid \mathcal{F}_1] = Y_1 + \mathbb{E}[Y_2] + \mathbb{E}[Y_3] = Y_1 + 0 + 0 = Y_1 = X_1$$
Continuous Time: $\mathcal{F}_s = \sigma(Y(u), 0 \le u \le s)$ represents the total history recorded up to time $s$. Any future increment $Y(t) - Y(s)$ (for $t > s$) is independent of $\mathcal{F}_s$.

3. Moment Generating Function & Derivation of $\mathbb{E}[X(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s]$

For $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(m, v)$, its MGF gives $\mathbb{E}[e^Z] = e^{m + \frac{1}{2}v}$.
Since $Y(t) - Y(s) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu(t-s), \sigma^2(t-s))$: $$\mathbb{E}\left[ e^{Y(t) - Y(s)} \right] = \exp\left( \left(\mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)(t-s) \right)$$
Conditional Expectation: $$\mathbb{E}[X(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = \mathbb{E}\left[ e^{Y(s)} e^{Y(t)-Y(s)} \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_s \right] = X(s) \cdot \mathbb{E}\left[ e^{Y(t)-Y(s)} \right] = X(s) \exp\left( \left(\mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)(t-s) \right)$$ Unconditional Expectation ($X(0) = x_0$): $$\mathbb{E}[X(t)] = x_0 \exp\left( \left(\mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t \right)$$

4. Martingale Property & Itô Drift Correction

GBM $X(t)$ is a Martingale ($\mathbb{E}[X(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = X(s)$) iff: $$\mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2} = 0 \implies \mu = -\frac{\sigma^2}{2}$$ The process $X(t) = \exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}t + \sigma W(t)\right)$ is the fundamental Exponential Martingale with constant expectation $\mathbb{E}[X(t)] = 1$.

5. Stock Price Modeling via i.i.d. Relative Returns

Let relative stock returns $Y_n = \frac{X_n}{X_{n-1}}$ be i.i.d. random variables.
$$X_n = Y_n Y_{n-1} \dots Y_1 X_0 \implies \log X_n = \sum_{i=1}^n \log(Y_i) + \log X_0$$ By the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), as $n \to \infty$, the sum $\sum \log Y_i$ converges to a Gaussian distribution, proving why stock price levels $X_n$ naturally follow Geometric Brownian Motion!

5. Jensen's Inequality: Why $\mu = -\frac{\sigma^2}{2}$ Cancels the Inflation

Since $e^z$ is a convex function, Jensen's inequality states: $$\mathbb{E}[e^Z] \ge e^{\mathbb{E}[Z]}$$ For $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 t)$ (mean 0): $$\mathbb{E}[e^Z] = e^{\frac{\sigma^2 t}{2}} > 1$$ Volatility $\sigma^2$ inflates the expectation above 1. The drift correction $\mu = -\frac{\sigma^2}{2}$ precisely cancels this inflation.
