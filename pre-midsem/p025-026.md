# Wiener Process Axioms, Lévy Characterization & Random Walk Limit

Constructing Brownian Motion from Axioms, Martingales, and Discrete Limits.

## 1. Classical Definition of Wiener Process

A stochastic process $\{W(t), t \ge 0\}$ is a **Standard Wiener Process** (Brownian Motion) iff:

- $W(0) = 0$ almost surely (a.s.).

- Sample paths $t \mapsto W(t, \omega)$ are continuous a.s.

- Increments are **independent** and **stationary**.

- For $0 \le s < t$, increments are Gaussian: $W(t) - W(s) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$.

## 2. Lévy's Martingale Characterization

Without assuming independent or Gaussian increments directly, a continuous process $W(t)$ with $W(0) = 0$ is a Wiener process iff:

- $W(t)$ is a martingale wrt natural filtration $\mathcal{F}_t$: $\mathbb{E}[W(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = W(s)$.

- $W(t)^2 - t$ is a martingale wrt $\mathcal{F}_t$: $\mathbb{E}[W(t)^2 - t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = W(s)^2 - s$.

**Conditional Variance Derivation:**

$$\mathbb{E}\left[(W(t) - W(s))^2 \;\middle|\; \mathcal{F}_s\right] = \mathbb{E}[W(t)^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_s] - 2W(s)\mathbb{E}[W(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s] + W(s)^2 = (W(s)^2 + t - s) - 2W(s)^2 + W(s)^2 = t - s$$

## 3. Microscopic Random Walk Construction

<!-- anim: walk path=uduudduu step=1 label="X_n" say="A random walk with tiny steps: the microscopic picture of Brownian motion." -->

Consider a discrete symmetric random walk with step size $\Delta x$ every time step $\Delta t$:

$$X(t) = \Delta x \sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor t / \Delta t \rfloor} X_i \quad \text{where } X_i = \pm 1 \text{ with equal prob } \frac{1}{2}$$

$$\mathbb{E}[X(t)] = 0, \quad \text{Var}(X(t)) = (\Delta x)^2 \cdot \frac{t}{\Delta t}$$

## 4. The Diffusion Scaling Limit & CLT

<!-- anim: clt ns=(4,16,64,256) say="Scaled correctly, the walk at time $t$ becomes normal: the CLT." -->

If $\Delta x = \Delta t \to 0$, $\text{Var}(X(t)) = t \Delta t \to 0$ (motion collapses). To preserve non-zero variance as step size vanishes, we must scale spatial steps as:

$$\Delta x = \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t} \implies \text{Var}(X(t)) \to \sigma^2 t$$

By the **Central Limit Theorem (CLT)**, as $\Delta t \to 0$ ($N = \frac{t}{\Delta t} \to \infty$), the standardized sum $\frac{S_N}{\sqrt{N}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. Multiplying by $c = \sigma \sqrt{t}$ scales the variance by $c^2 = \sigma^2 t$:

$$X(t) = \sigma \sqrt{t} \cdot \left( \frac{S_N}{\sqrt{N}} \right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 t)$$
