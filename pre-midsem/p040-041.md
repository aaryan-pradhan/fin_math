# Hitting Times, Reflection Principle & Running Maximum

Wiener Process Stopping Times, Strong Markov Symmetry, and Running Max Distributions.

## 1. Hitting Time Definition

For standard Brownian motion $W(t)$, the first passage time (hitting time) to level $a$ is:

$$T_a = \inf\{t \ge 0 : W(t) = a\}$$

$T_a$ is a stopping time because its occurrence depends only on path history up to time $t$.

## 2. The Reflection Principle

<!-- anim: reflection a=1 T=2 say="The reflection principle: after the path hits $a$, its mirror image is equally likely." -->

There are two equivalent ways to understand the Reflection Principle: **Analytical (conditioning via Strong Markov Property)** and **Geometric (path-wise reflection)**.

## Method A: Analytical (Strong Markov Property)

For $a > 0$, partition the probability of being above $a$ at time $t$ using the law of total probability:

$$\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) = \mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a \mid T_a \le t)\mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) + \mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a \mid T_a > t)\mathbb{P}(T_a > t)$$

- $\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a \mid T_a > t) = 0$ because if the path never hit $a$ by time $t$, it must be strictly below $a$.

- By the **Strong Markov Property**, at the stopping time $T_a$, the process restarts. The post-$T_a$ increment process $B(u) = W(T_a + u) - W(T_a)$ for $u \ge 0$ is a new standard Brownian motion independent of the history before $T_a$.

- By standard Gaussian symmetry, $B(t - T_a) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t - T_a)$ is symmetric about $0$. Thus:

$$\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a \mid T_a \le t) = \mathbb{P}(a + B(t - T_a) \ge a \mid T_a \le t) = \mathbb{P}(B(t - T_a) \ge 0) = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) \implies \mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) = 2\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a)$$

## Method B: Geometric (Path-wise Reflection)

Let $\omega$ be a sample path that hits level $a$ at stopping time $T_a(\omega) \le t$. Define the **reflected path** $W^*(u)$ by reflecting the trajectory across the line $y = a$ after time $T_a$:

$$W^*(u) = \begin{cases} W(u) & \text{for } u \le T_a \\ 2a - W(u) & \text{for } u > T_a \end{cases}$$

- **Equiprobability:** Since standard Brownian motion increments are symmetric about 0, the reflected path $W^*(u)$ is just as likely to occur as the original path $W(u)$.

- **Bijection:** Every path that hits $a$ and ends up above $a$ at time $t$ ($W(t) \ge a$) maps uniquely to a reflected path that hits $a$ and ends up below $a$ ($W^*(t) < a$).

- Thus:

$$\mathbb{P}(T_a \le t, W(t) \ge a) = \mathbb{P}(T_a \le t, W(t) < a)$$

Since $\{W(t) \ge a\} \subset \{T_a \le t\}$, we can drop the $T_a \le t$ condition on the left side:

$$\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) = \mathbb{P}(T_a \le t, W(t) < a)$$

The total probability of hitting $a$ by time $t$ is the sum of paths ending above $a$ and paths ending below $a$:

$$\mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) = \mathbb{P}(T_a \le t, W(t) \ge a) + \mathbb{P}(T_a \le t, W(t) < a) = \mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) + \mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) = 2\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a)$$

## 3. Probability Distribution of $T_a$

Using standard Gaussian integration with substitution $y = x / \sqrt{t}$:

$$\mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{|a|/\sqrt{t}}^{\infty} e^{-\frac{y^2}{2}} \, dy = 2\left[ 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{|a|}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \right]$$

Differentiating the CDF with respect to $t$ gives the PDF of $T_a$ (Lévy distribution):

$$f(t) = \frac{|a|}{\sqrt{2\pi t^3}} e^{-\frac{a^2}{2t}} \quad (t > 0)$$

## 4. The Running Maximum $M(t)$

Define the running maximum up to time $t$ as:

$$M(t) = \max_{0 \le s \le t} W(s)$$

The event that the maximum is at least $a$ is equivalent to the hitting time being at or before $t$:

$$\{M(t) \ge a\} \iff \{T_a \le t\}$$

$$\mathbb{P}(M(t) \ge a) = 2\mathbb{P}(W(t) \ge a) = 2\left[ 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \right] \quad (a > 0)$$

## 5. Certain Hitting Time ($\mathbb{P}(T_a < \infty) = 1$)

$$\mathbb{P}(T_a < \infty) = \lim_{t \to \infty} \mathbb{P}(T_a \le t) = \lim_{t \to \infty} 2\left[ 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{|a|}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \right] = 2\left[ 1 - \Phi(0) \right] = 2\left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = 1$$

Standard Brownian motion will hit any level $a$ almost surely, though the expected time $\mathbb{E}[T_a] = \infty$.
