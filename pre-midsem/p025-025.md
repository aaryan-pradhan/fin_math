# Standard Brownian Motion Density & The Diffusion Equation

Verification of the Heat/Diffusion PDE satisfied by the transition density of Wiener process.

## 1. The Density of Standard Brownian Motion

<!-- anim: density_spread say="The density of $W_t$ is $N(0,t)$: it flattens and spreads as $t$ grows." -->

For fixed time $t > 0$, standard Brownian motion $W(t)$ is Gaussian distributed with mean $0$ and variance $t$: $W(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t)$. Its probability density function $f_{W(t)}(x)$ is given by:

$$f(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}}$$

## 2. Time Derivative $\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}$

Express $f(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} t^{-1/2} e^{-x^2 / (2t)}$. Applying the product rule and chain rule with respect to $t$:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \left[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(t^{-1/2}\right) e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} + t^{-1/2} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}}\right) \right] \\ &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \left[ -\frac{1}{2} t^{-3/2} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} + t^{-1/2} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} \cdot \frac{x^2}{2t^2} \right] \\ &= \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} \left[ \frac{x^2}{t^{5/2}} - \frac{1}{t^{3/2}} \right] \end{aligned}$$

## 3. Spatial Derivatives $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ and $\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}$

**First derivative:**

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} \left(-\frac{x}{t}\right) = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi} t^{3/2}} x e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}}$$

**Second derivative:** Apply the product rule to $x e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}}$:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left[ x e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} \right] = 1 \cdot e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} + x \left(-\frac{x}{t} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}}\right) = e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} \left( 1 - \frac{x^2}{t} \right)$$

Multiplying by $-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi} t^{3/2}}$ yields:

$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2t}} \left[ \frac{x^2}{t^{5/2}} - \frac{1}{t^{3/2}} \right]$$

## 4. PDE Equivalence

Comparing $\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}$ and $\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}$:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}$$

Hence, the probability density function $f_{W(t)}(x)$ of standard Brownian motion is the **fundamental solution** to the 1D diffusion equation with diffusion coefficient $D = \frac{1}{2}$.

## 5. Key Summary

- Microscopic level: Sample paths $t \mapsto W(t, \omega)$ are non-differentiable and random.

- Macroscopic ensemble level: The probability density $f(x, t)$ evolves deterministically and smoothly via heat diffusion.
