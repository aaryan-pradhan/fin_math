# Gambling Strategies & Fairness

My understanding, built from scratch.

## 1. The Game Process ($\Xi_n$)

Let $\eta_n$ be the winnings/losses **per unit stake** in game $n$. Total winnings after $n$ games with a unit stake: $\Xi_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \eta_k$. Information available after $n$ games: $\mathcal{F}_n = \sigma(\eta_1, \ldots, \eta_n)$.

| Condition | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $E(\Xi_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) = \Xi_{n-1}$ | Martingale | Fair game (expected change = 0) |
| $E(\Xi_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \geq \Xi_{n-1}$ | Sub-Martingale | Favorable to you (expected change $\geq 0$) |
| $E(\Xi_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \leq \Xi_{n-1}$ | Super-Martingale | Unfavorable to you (expected change $\leq 0$) |

- **Fair coin:** $\eta = \pm1$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each, so $E\eta = 0$ and it's a MG. Results $+1, -1, +1$ give $\Xi = 1, 0, 1$.

- **Roulette, betting on red** (18 red out of 37 pockets): $\eta = +1$ with probability $\tfrac{18}{37}$, $-1$ with probability $\tfrac{19}{37}$. $E\eta = -\tfrac1{37} \approx -0.027$, a super-MG. You lose about 2.7 paise per rupee staked.

- **Loaded coin in your favour,** $p = 0.6$: $E\eta = 0.2$, a sub-MG.

## 2. The Gambling Strategy ($\alpha_n$)

In game $n$, your stake is $\alpha_n$. **Crucial Condition:** $\alpha_n$ must be **$\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$-measurable** (predictable).

⚠️ If $\alpha_n$ were $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable, you would be placing bets *after* knowing the outcome (crystal ball / insider trading). The math requires predictable strategies to model reality correctly.

- Predictable ✅: $\alpha_n = 2$ if you won game $n-1$, else 1. $\alpha_n = n$ (deterministic). Doubling: $\alpha_n = 2^{(\text{losses in a row so far})}$.

- Not predictable ❌: $\alpha_n = \mathbb{1}\{\eta_n = +1\}$, "bet only on the games you'll win". Then $Z_n$ = number of wins $\ge 0$, and $E[Z_1] = \tfrac12 > 0$ in a fair game. The crystal ball beats fairness.

## 3. Total Wealth Process ($Z_n$)

Your total wealth after $n$ games with strategy $\alpha$:

$$Z_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k \eta_k = \sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k (\Xi_k - \Xi_{k-1})$$

Recursive form: $Z_n = Z_{n-1} + \alpha_n(\Xi_n - \Xi_{n-1})$.

Doubling after each loss. Results $\eta = +1, -1, -1, +1$.

| $n$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\alpha_n$ | 1 | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| $\alpha_n\eta_n$ | +1 | −1 | −2 | +4 |
| $Z_n$ | 1 | 0 | −2 | 2 |

After the win at game 1 the stake resets to 1. It doubles after each loss (1 → 2 → 4).

## 4. Preservation Theorem

**Part 1 (Martingale):** If the game is fair ($\Xi_n$ is MG) and your strategy $\alpha$ is bounded, then your total wealth $Z_n$ is also a MG. *(You cannot invent a betting system to beat a fair casino).*

**Proof:**

$$E(Z_n \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) = E\big( Z_{n-1} + \alpha_n(\Xi_n - \Xi_{n-1}) \;\big|\; \mathcal{F}_{n-1} \big)$$

Since $Z_{n-1}$ and $\alpha_n$ are $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$-measurable, they pull out:

$$= Z_{n-1} + \alpha_n \cdot \underbrace{E( \Xi_n - \Xi_{n-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1})}_{=0 \text{ (since } \Xi \text{ is MG)}}$$

$$= Z_{n-1} \quad \checkmark$$

Numeric check, two fair games: $\alpha_1 = 1$, and $\alpha_2 = 2$ after a loss, 1 after a win.

| results | $Z_2$ |
|---|---|
| W W | $1 + 1 = 2$ |
| W L | $1 - 1 = 0$ |
| L W | $-1 + 2 = 1$ |
| L L | $-1 - 2 = -3$ |

$E[Z_2] = \tfrac14(2 + 0 + 1 - 3) = 0$ ✅. The strategy changes the *shape* of the outcomes, not the mean.

**Part 2 (Sub-Martingale):** If the game is favorable ($\Xi_n$ is Sub-MG) and your strategy is bounded and **non-negative** ($\alpha_n \geq 0$), then $Z_n$ is also a Sub-MG.

**Why $\alpha_n \geq 0$?** If $\Xi_n$ is a Sub-MG, $E(\Xi_n - \Xi_{n-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \geq 0$. If you were allowed to take a negative position (short selling, $\alpha_n < 0$), you would multiply a negative stake by a positive expected gain, resulting in an expected loss! To maintain the favorable property, you must stick to long positions.

Loaded coin, $E\eta = 0.2$. Stake $\alpha = 3$: expected gain per game $3\times 0.2 = +0.6$. Stake $\alpha = -3$ (betting against the favourable side): $-3\times 0.2 = -0.6$.

## Practice Questions

**Q1.** Roulette on red, stake ₹10 for each of 37 spins. Expected total?

Answer

$37\times 10\times(-\tfrac1{37}) = -₹10$.

**Q2.** Fair coin, stake $\alpha_n = n$ in game $n$. Find $E[Z_{10}]$ and $\text{Var}(Z_{10})$.

Answer

$Z_{10} = \sum n\eta_n$. $E = 0$. $\text{Var} = \sum n^2\,\text{Var}(\eta) = 1 + 4 + \cdots + 100 = 385$. Same mean, much bigger risk.

**Q3.** Doubling until the first win, capped at 3 games. Give the distribution of the final wealth and check fairness.

Answer

A win at game 1, 2 or 3 gives $+1$, with probability $\tfrac12 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac18 = \tfrac78$. Losing all three gives $-(1 + 2 + 4) = -7$, probability $\tfrac18$. $E = \tfrac78 - \tfrac78 = 0$ ✅

**Q4.** Is $\alpha_n = |\Xi_{n-1}| + 1$ allowed in the preservation theorem?

Answer

Predictable ✅ (it uses only $\Xi_{n-1}$). Bounded for each $n$: $|\Xi_{n-1}| \le n-1$, so $\alpha_n \le n$ ✅. Hence $Z_n$ is a MG in a fair game.

**Q5.** Loaded coin ($p = 0.6$). Which make $Z_n$ a sub-MG: (a) $\alpha_n = 2$, (b) $\alpha_n = -1$, (c) $\alpha_n = \mathbb{1}\{\text{won game } n-1\}$?

Answer

(a) Yes. (b) No, it's a super-MG (drift $-0.2$ per game). (c) Yes: predictable, bounded, $\ge 0$.
