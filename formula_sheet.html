FinMath Formula Sheet

Every formula worth memorizing — probability theory, martingales & stochastic processes (chapters 01–30 + joint distribution note)

1.Measure-Theoretic Foundations

σ-Field Axioms

$\emptyset \in \mathcal{F}$;   $A \in \mathcal{F} \Rightarrow A^c \in \mathcal{F}$;   $A_1, A_2, \ldots \in \mathcal{F} \Rightarrow \bigcup_i A_i \in \mathcal{F}$
The collection of events you're allowed to assign probability to.

Probability Measure Axioms

$P(\Omega) = 1$;   $A_i$ disjoint $\Rightarrow P\left(\bigcup_i A_i\right) = \sum_i P(A_i)$

Filtration

$\mathcal{F}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_2 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq \mathcal{F}$
$\mathcal{F}_t$ = all information observable by time $t$; nested and increasing.

ℱ-Measurability

$X$ is $\mathcal{F}$-measurable $\iff [X \in B] = X^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{F}\ \ \forall B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$
Guarantees $P(X \in B)$ is well-defined; a random variable is a measurable function.

σ-Field Generated by a R.V.

$\sigma(\xi) = \{\xi^{-1}(B) : B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\}$
Smallest σ-field making $\xi$ measurable; the events decidable from $\xi(\omega)$ alone.

Doob–Dynkin Lemma

$\eta$ is $\sigma(\xi)$-measurable $\iff \eta = f(\xi)$ for some Borel $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$
$\eta$ is fully determined by $\xi$'s value — no extra randomness.

Adapted Process

$(X_t)$ adapted to $\mathbb{F} \iff X_t$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable $\ \forall t$
$X_t$ can be computed from information available by time $t$ — no look-ahead.

Borel-Cantelli Lemma

$\sum_{i=1}^\infty P(A_i) < \infty \implies P(\limsup_n A_n) = 0$
Almost surely, only finitely many $A_i$ occur — eventually they stop forever.

Push-Forward (Distribution) Measure

$P_\xi(B) = P(\xi \in B), \quad B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$
Transports probability from abstract $\Omega$ onto the real line.

Cumulative Distribution Function

$F_\xi(x) = P(\xi \leq x) = P_\xi((-\infty, x])$
Non-decreasing, right-continuous, $F(-\infty)=0$, $F(+\infty)=1$.

Density (Absolutely Continuous r.v.)

$P(\xi \in B) = \int_B f_\xi(x)\,dx$
Single points have probability 0: $P(\xi = x_0) = 0$.

Discrete Distribution

$P(\xi \in B) = \sum_{x_i \in B} P(\xi = x_i)$

Joint Density in $\mathbb{R}^n$

$P\big((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\in B\big) = \int\!\cdots\!\int_B f_{\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,dx_1\cdots dx_n$
2.Expectation, Integration & Change of Variables

Expectation as Lebesgue Integral

$E(\xi) = \int_\Omega \xi\,dP, \quad \text{requires } \int_\Omega |\xi|\,dP < \infty$
$\xi$ integrable ($\xi \in L^1$) iff its absolute value has finite expectation.

Expectation of an Indicator

$E(\mathbf{1}_A) = \int_\Omega \mathbf{1}_A\,dP = P(A)$

Expectation of a Simple Function

$\eta = \sum_{i=1}^n \eta_i \mathbf{1}_{A_i} \implies E(\eta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \eta_i P(A_i)$
$A_i$ pairwise disjoint.

Change of Variables for Expectation

$E(h(\xi)) = \int_\Omega h(\xi(\omega))\,dP(\omega) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} h(x)\,dP_\xi(x)$
Shifts integration from abstract $\Omega$ to $\mathbb{R}$ via the push-forward measure.

Change of Variables — Continuous Case

$E(h(\xi)) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(x)\,f_\xi(x)\,dx$

Change of Variables — Discrete Case

$E(h(\xi)) = \sum_i h(x_i)\,P(\xi = x_i)$
3.Conditional Probability, Independence & Conditional Expectation

Conditional Probability

$P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}, \quad P(B) \neq 0$

Law of Total Probability

$P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty P(A \mid B_i)\,P(B_i)$
$\{B_i\}$ a countable partition of $\Omega$.

Independence (Two Events)

$P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B) \iff P(A\mid B) = P(A)$

Mutual Independence ($n$ Events)

$P(A_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap A_{i_k}) = P(A_{i_1})\cdots P(A_{i_k})$ for every subcollection
Pairwise independence is not enough; requires $2^n - n - 1$ equations total.

Conditional Expectation — Defining Properties

$Y = E(X \mid \mathcal{G})$:  (1) $\sigma(Y) \subseteq \mathcal{G}$;  (2) $\int_G Y\,dP = \int_G X\,dP\ \ \forall G \in \mathcal{G}$
$Y$ is the unique (a.s.) $\mathcal{G}$-measurable "best average" of $X$ given information $\mathcal{G}$.

Conditional Expectation on a Partition

$E(X \mid \mathcal{G})(\omega) = \dfrac{\int_{A_i} X\,dP}{P(A_i)} = \dfrac{E[X \cdot \mathbf{1}_{A_i}]}{P(A_i)}, \ \ \omega \in A_i$
$\mathcal{G} = \sigma(A_1,\ldots,A_k)$, a finite partition.

Tower Property (Law of Iterated Expectations)

$E\big(E(X \mid \mathcal{G})\big) = E(X)$

Conditional Expectation — Linearity

$E(aX + bY \mid \mathcal{G}) = aE(X\mid\mathcal{G}) + bE(Y\mid\mathcal{G})$

Taking Out What Is Known

$E(ZX \mid \mathcal{G}) = Z\cdot E(X \mid \mathcal{G})$
$Z$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable.

Independence Rule

$X$ independent of $\mathcal{G} \implies E(X \mid \mathcal{G}) = E(X)$
4.Martingales — Core Definitions & Theorems

Martingale

$E[X_{t+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = X_t \quad (P\text{-a.s.})$
Adapted + integrable + "fair game": tomorrow's expectation equals today's value.

Submartingale / Supermartingale

Sub: $E[X_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_t] \geq X_t$    Super: $E[X_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_t] \leq X_t$
Sub = grows in expectation (player-favored); Super = shrinks (house-favored).

Multi-Step Martingale Property

$E[X_{t+s} \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = X_t \quad \forall s \geq 1$
Proved by iterating the tower property one step at a time.

Constant Unconditional Mean

$E[X_t] = E[X_0] \quad \forall t \geq 0$
Sub-MG: $E[X_t] \geq E[X_0]$.
5.Random Walks & the Binomial Model

Additive Random Walk — Martingale Condition

$X_n = \sum_{j=1}^n Y_j, \quad$ MG $\iff E[Y_1] = 0$
Sub-MG $\iff E[Y_1]\geq 0$; Super-MG $\iff E[Y_1]\leq 0$.

Geometric Random Walk — Martingale Condition

$X_n = X_0\,e^{Y_1+\cdots+Y_n}, \quad$ MG $\iff E[e^{Y_1}] = 1$

Log-Normal Increments — Drift Correction

$Y_j \sim N(\mu,\sigma^2) \implies E[e^{Y_1}] = e^{\mu + \frac12\sigma^2}$
MG $\iff \mu = -\tfrac12\sigma^2$
The canonical drift correction that reappears throughout GBM and Black–Scholes.

Binomial Asset Price

$S_t = v_t S_{t-1}, \quad v_t \in \{u, d\}, \quad 0

Binomial Stock — Martingale Condition

$S_t$ is MG $\iff p\,u + (1-p)\,d = 1$
$p = P(v_{t+1}=u)$.
6.Stopping Times & Optimal Stopping

Stopping Time — Definition

$\{\tau \leq t\} \in \mathcal{F}_t \ \ \forall t \iff \{\tau = t\} \in \mathcal{F}_t \ \ \forall t$
Whether you've stopped by time $t$ is decidable using only information up to $t$.

Stopped Process

$Z_t = X_{t \wedge \tau} = \sum_{k=0}^t X_k\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau=k\}} + X_{t+1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau>t\}}$
Freezes the process at whatever value it had when $\tau$ occurred.

Stopped-Process Martingale Preservation

$(X_t)$ MG $\implies (Z_t) = (X_{t\wedge\tau})$ is also MG
Type is preserved: MG→MG, Sub-MG→Sub-MG, Super-MG→Super-MG.

Optional Stopping Theorem

$(X_t)$ MG, $\tau \leq c$ a.s. (bounded) $\implies E[X_\tau] = E[X_0]$
No stopping strategy can beat a fair game. Fails if $\tau$ is unbounded.

Predictable Gambling Strategy

Stake $\alpha_n$ must be $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$-measurable
You bet before seeing the outcome — no crystal ball.

Wealth-Process Preservation

$\Xi_n$ MG, $\alpha$ bounded $\implies Z_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \alpha_k(\Xi_k-\Xi_{k-1})$ is MG
Sub-MG case additionally needs $\alpha_n \geq 0$.
7.Markov Decision Processes & Dynamic Programming

Stationary Policy

$a_t = f(X_t)$
Action depends only on the current state, never on time or history.

Value Function

$V_n(i) = \max_a\, E\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^n R(X_t,a_t)\ \middle|\ X_1=i\right]$
Max expected reward starting in state $i$ with $n$ steps remaining.

Bellman Equation

$V_n(i) = \max_a \left\{ R(i,a) + \sum_j P_{ij}(a)\,V_{n-1}(j) \right\}$
Immediate reward + expected optimal future reward; solved by backward induction from $V_0(i)=0$.
8.American Derivatives

American Derivative Security

Adapted process $(X_t)_{t\leq T}$, $\ X_t \geq 0$
Right, not obligation, to exercise at any $t \leq T$; payoff $X_\tau$ if exercised, $X_T$ if expired.
9.Standard Brownian Motion — Definition & Canonical Martingales

Wiener Process Axioms

$W_0=0$ a.s.; continuous paths a.s.; independent increments; $W_t - W_s \sim N(0, t-s)$

Lévy's Martingale Characterization

$W_t$ MG and $W_t^2 - t$ MG $\implies (W_t)$ is a Wiener process
Alternative to the four classical axioms — needs no independence/Gaussian assumption directly.

Canonical Martingale 1 — Linear

$E[W_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = W_s$

Canonical Martingale 2 — Quadratic

$E[W_t^2 - t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = W_s^2 - s$
$(W_t^2)$ alone is a submartingale: $E[W_t^2\mid\mathcal{F}_s]=W_s^2+(t-s)$.

Canonical Martingale 3 — Exponential

$Z_t = e^{uW_t - \frac12 u^2 t}, \quad E[Z_t \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = Z_s, \quad E[Z_t]=1$

Density of $W(t)$

$f(x,t) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\,e^{-x^2/2t}$

Diffusion (Heat) Equation

$\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial t} = \dfrac12\,\dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}$
The BM density is the fundamental solution with diffusion coefficient $D=\tfrac12$.

Diffusive Scaling Limit

$\Delta x = \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t} \implies X(t) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,\sigma^2 t)$
Random walk converges to BM only under this specific $\sqrt{\Delta t}$ step scaling (CLT).

Brownian Motion as Gaussian Process

$m(t) = 0, \quad K(s,t) = \operatorname{Cov}(W(s),W(t)) = s \wedge t = \min(s,t)$
10.Hitting Times, Reflection Principle & Running Maximum

Hitting Time

$T_a = \inf\{t \geq 0 : W(t) = a\}$

Reflection Principle Result

$P(T_a \leq t) = 2\,P(W(t) \geq a) = 2\left[1 - \Phi\!\left(\dfrac{|a|}{\sqrt t}\right)\right]$

Reflected Path

$W^*(u) = \begin{cases} W(u) & u \leq T_a \\ 2a - W(u) & u > T_a \end{cases}$
Equiprobable to the original path; the bijective trick behind the reflection principle.

Hitting-Time Density (Lévy Distribution)

$f(t) = \dfrac{|a|}{\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}\,e^{-a^2/2t}, \quad t>0$

Certain Hitting, Infinite Expectation

$P(T_a < \infty) = 1, \qquad E[T_a] = \infty$

Running Maximum

$M(t) = \max_{0 \leq s \leq t} W(s)$
$\{M(t) \geq a\} \iff \{T_a \leq t\}$.

Distribution of the Running Maximum

$P(M(t) > a) = 2\left[1 - \Phi\!\left(\dfrac{a}{\sqrt t}\right)\right], \quad a>0$

Joint $W(t)$/$M(t)$ — Reflection Formula

$P(W(t) \leq a-y,\ M(t) > a) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\dfrac{a+y}{\sqrt t}\right)$
Requires $a > 0$.

Joint $W(t)$/$M(t)$ — Half-Line Form

$P(W(t) \leq b,\ M(t) \geq c) = 1 - \Phi\!\left(\dfrac{2c-b}{\sqrt t}\right)$

Joint $W(t)$/$M(t)$ — Rectangle (Disjoint Intervals)

$P(a{<}W(t){\leq}b,\ c{<}M(t){\leq}d) = \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{2d-b}{\sqrt t}\right) - \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{2c-b}{\sqrt t}\right) + \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{2c-a}{\sqrt t}\right) - \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{2d-a}{\sqrt t}\right)$
$I=(a,b]$ entirely below $J=(c,d]$, $c>0$.
11.Brownian Motion with Drift, Gambler's Ruin & Exit Probabilities

Brownian Motion with Drift

$X(t) = \mu t + W(t) \sim N(\mu t,\, t)$

Discrete Gambler's Ruin — Unfair Game

$P_i = \dfrac{1-(q/p)^i}{1-(q/p)^N}, \quad p \neq \tfrac12$
$P_i$ = probability of reaching $N$ before $0$, starting from $i$.

Discrete Gambler's Ruin — Fair Game

$P_i = \dfrac{i}{N}, \quad p = \tfrac12$

Infinite Bankroll Limit

$\lim_{N\to\infty} P_i = \begin{cases} 1-(q/p)^i & p > \tfrac12 \\ 0 & p \leq \tfrac12 \end{cases}$
Against an infinite bankroll, ruin is a.s. certain unless you have a genuine positive edge.

BM with Drift — Two-Barrier Exit Probability

$P(x) = P(\text{hit } A \text{ before } {-}B \mid X(0){=}x) = \dfrac{1 - e^{-2\mu(x+B)}}{1 - e^{-2\mu(A+B)}}$
Continuous-time analogue of discrete gambler's ruin.

Negative Drift — Max Ever Reached

$P(\max_{t\geq0} X(t) \geq A) = e^{2\mu A}, \quad \mu<0$
$M = \sup_t X(t)$ is Exponentially distributed with rate $-2\mu$.

Zero-Drift Exit Probability

$P(x) = \dfrac{x+B}{A+B}$

Optimal Exercise Threshold (Drift $-d$)

$x^* = A + \dfrac{1}{2d}, \qquad f(x^*) = \dfrac{1}{2d}\,e^{-2dA-1}$
Maximizes $f(x)=(x-A)e^{-2dx}$, expected gain from exercising an option at target $x$.
12.Brownian Bridge & Conditional Distributions

Standard Brownian Bridge

$[W(s) \mid W(t)=B] \sim N\!\left(\dfrac{s}{t}B,\ \dfrac{s(t-s)}{t}\right)$
$0 \leq s < t$.

General BM Bridge ($Y=\sigma W$)

$[Y(s) \mid Y(t)=C] \sim N\!\left(\dfrac{s}{t}C,\ \sigma^2\dfrac{s(t-s)}{t}\right)$
Bridge variance vanishes as $s\to0$ or $s\to t$ — certainty at the endpoints.
13.Geometric Brownian Motion

GBM Definition

$X(t) = e^{Y(t)}, \quad Y(t) \sim N(\mu t, \sigma^2 t)$
$X(t) > 0$ always; $\log X(t)$ is Gaussian (log-normal price model).

Conditional Expectation of GBM

$E[X(t) \mid \mathcal{F}_s] = X(s)\,\exp\!\left[\left(\mu+\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)(t-s)\right]$

Unconditional Expectation of GBM

$E[X(t)] = x_0\,\exp\!\left[\left(\mu+\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t\right]$

GBM Martingale (Drift) Condition

$\mu = -\dfrac{\sigma^2}{2} \implies X(t)=\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}t+\sigma W(t)\right),\ E[X(t)]=1$
Volatility inflates $E[e^Z]$ above 1 (Jensen: $E[e^Z]\geq e^{E[Z]}$); this drift term exactly cancels it.
14.Multivariate Normal & Gaussian Processes

MVN Construction

$\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{Z} + \boldsymbol{\mu}, \quad \mathbf{Z}\sim N(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_n)$

$Y = \mathbf{t}^T\mathbf{X} \sim N\!\left(\mathbf{t}^T\boldsymbol{\mu},\ \mathbf{t}^T\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{t}\right)$

1D Normal Exponential Moment

$Y \sim N(\mu_Y,\sigma_Y^2) \implies E[e^Y] = \exp\!\left(\mu_Y + \tfrac12\sigma_Y^2\right)$

Joint MGF of MVN

$\phi_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{t}) = \exp\!\left(\mathbf{t}^T\boldsymbol{\mu} + \tfrac12\mathbf{t}^T\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{t}\right)$

MVN Linear Transformation
